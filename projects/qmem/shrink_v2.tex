\subsection{Shrinking}
\label{subsection:shrink}
\inb{\textbf{Rewrite.}} We are about to prove that the sweep rule shrinks fintie errors.  
The following claim relates the local maximum points of the potential walls $L_{1}, \ldots, L_{d}$ in an assignment $z$ to the structure of the syndrome they observe. Later, we will use that relation to infer that the decoding step of the sweep rule can't spread the error to a vertex\footnote{To faces whose vertices have higher values of $L_{1}, \ldots, L_{d}$ than the current global maximum values.} with a higher value than the current global maximum of $L_{1}, \ldots, L_{d}$. In other words, the error does not spread in the positive directions.

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:edge_pole}
Let $z$ and $\xi$ be $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chains, such that $\xi$ results from the application of the sweep rule on $z$. For any generator $g \in S$, the maximal values of $L_{g}$ in $\xi$ and $\partial^{-} \xi$ are lower than the maximal values in $z$ and $\partial^{-} z$. Namely:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    & \max \left\{ L_{g}(u) : u \in \xi \right\}  \le  \max \big\{ L_{g}(u) : u \in z \big\} \\
    & \max \left\{ L_{g}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} \xi \right\}  \le  \max \big\{ L_{g}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} z \big\} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}

\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  First, we argue that for a vertex $v$ in $\partial^{-} z$, and a generator $g \in S$, such that $v$ is a local maximum of $L_{g}$ in either $z$ or $\partial^{-} z$, it implies that $gv \notin \partial^{-} z$. The contradiction where $v$ is a maximum of $L_{g}$ in $z$ follows immediately, since containment in $\partial^{-} z$ implies containment in $z$. Where $v$ is a maximum in $\partial^{-} z$, suppose through contradiction that $gv \in \partial^{-} z$, then
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      L_{g}(gv) = (gv)_{g} = 1 + v_{g} = 1 + L_{g}(v)
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
In contradiction to $v$ being a local maximum of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-} z$.

For a vertex $v \in \partial^{-}z$ denote the correction it applies by $\varphi_{v} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}} $, so 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \xi = \sum_{v \in \partial^{-}z}\varphi_{v} + z \ \ \text{ and } \ \ \partial^{-}\xi = \sum_{v \in \partial^{-}z}\partial^{-}\varphi_{v} + \partial^{-}z
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
So for any vertex $u \in \xi/z$, there is a vertex $v \in \partial^{-}z$ such that $u \in \varphi_{v}$. Moreover, since $\phi_{v}$ is a rooted chain, it follows that $L_{g}(u) \le L_{g}(v) + 1$, where the equality holds when $g$ doesn't belong to the generator's subset of the face $\varphi_{v}$. Similarly for $u \in \partial^{-}\xi/\partial^{-}z$. 

Now, combining with the sweep rule's first condition, which restricts the correction applied by a vertex to have a vertex support that is contained in the positive syndrome it sees, we have that for any vertex $v$ which is a local maximum of $L_{g}$ (in either $z$ or $\partial^{-}z$), any $u \in \varphi_{v}$ satisfies $L_{g}(u) = L_{g}(v)$. In other words, $\xi$ and $\partial^{-}\xi$ don't contain vertices of the form $gv$ where $v$ is a local maximum of $L_{g}$ in $z$ and $\partial^{-}z$, and the desired inequalities follow.
\end{proof}


Next, we prove that for a finite $X$-error, the sweep-cycle advances the corner (the maximum of $L_{d+1}$) of its reduced form. We assume we are given a finite error in its reduced form, and therefore whose corner sees a syndrome, and show that the sweep-rule application excludes the corner. In other words, the sweep-rule shrinks $X$-errors.

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:sweepshrink}
Let $z$ be a finite reduced $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain, and let $\xi$ be the reduced result of applying the sweep rule to it. Then:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For any generator $g \in S$: $\max \big\{ L_{g}(v) : v \in z \big\} \ge \max \big\{ L_{g}(v) : v \in \xi \big\}  $
    \item And for $L_{d+1}$ : $ \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(v)  : v \in z \big\} > \max \big\{  L_{d+1}(v) : v \in \xi \big\} + 1$
  \end{enumerate}
  In particular, $\Size{\xi} \le \Size{z} - 1$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  The first inequality was proved in \Cref{claim:edge_pole}, so it's left to prove the second. Denote by $\varphi$ the applied correction and by $v$ the vertex on which $L_{d+1}$ gets the maximal value in $z$, if there are more then one vertex then choose $v$ arbiterly. Since $z$ is reduced, then by \Cref{claim:reducederr} it holds that $v \in \partial^{-} z$. Moreover, since $v$ is a local max of $L_{d+1}$ in $z$ the two follows: 
  \begin{enumerate}
      \item \Cref{claim:findcor} guarantees that $\partial^{-} \varphi|_{v} = \partial^{-} z|_{v}$. Thus, adding $\varphi|_{v}$ to $z$ excludes $v$ from the boundary.
      \item For any other vertex $u \in z$, there is no face $\rotfv{u}{S^{\prime}} \in z$ such that $v \in \rotfv{u}{S^{\prime}}$. Otherwise, it would contradict $v$ being a local maximum of $L_{d+1}$ in $z$. Thus, $v \notin \textbf{Str}_{0+}(u, \partial^{-} z)$, so by \Cref{claim:findcor}, $v \notin \varphi|_{u}$.
  \end{enumerate}
Combining the two, we get that $v \notin \partial^{-} (z + \varphi)$, so the reduced form of $z + \varphi$ does not contain $v$. Namely, $v \notin \xi$. Repeating the above for all the other vertices which get the maximal $L_{d+1}$-value in $z$ yields that $L_{d+1}(\xi) \le L_{d+1}(z) - 1$.

\end{proof}
We finished analyzing the first part of the sweep-cycle that acts in the computational basis. In the next section, we show that the second part of the cycle shrinks reduced phase errors.
\subsubsection{The Sweep-Rule Phase in the Dual Code}

\inb{\textbf{Rewrite.}} Now, we will think of the binary assignment $z \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$ as the positions of the $Z$-errors, after permuting the qubits by the $\phi$-transpose. Namely, $\partial^{-}z$ is the assignment boundary that we would like to shrink, and $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$ is its source prior to the reordering. 

In this stage of the sweep-cycle, the sweep-rule acts in opposite directions. 
%We will keep $S$ for denoting the 'current' positive directions relative
Thus, it will be sufficient to show that the extremities of the potential walls in $\partial^{-} z$ mirror the extremities of the potential walls of $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. Subsequently, since the sweep-rule shrinks $\partial^{-}z$ (with sign adjustments), we have that the $\phi$-transpose of the yielded assignment also admits a shrunk coboundary relative to $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. 


However, we would not be able to show an exact mirroring, yet we show that the situation is close, and that would be enough. Specifically, we will show that for $g \in S$, any of the extremities of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-} z$ are higher (sent closer) than the mirrored extremities of $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. For $L_{d+1}$, the extremities of $\partial^{-}z$ have an equal $L_{d+1}$ value to the mirrored extremities in $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$ plus $d - d^{\prime} - 1$.

\inb{\textbf{Consdering redefine $\phi$ to flip the positive direction, such the root of $\phi(\rotfs{})$ is the up right corner. }}

\label{subsection:dualcode}
\begin{claim}
Let $z$ be an assignment. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For any generator $g \in S$, and potential wall $L_{g}$ it holds that: 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \min_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{-}z }L_{g}(v^{\prime}) \ge  - \max_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z) }  L_{g}(v^{\prime}) 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
     \item The following equality holds:
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \max_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{-}z }L_{d+1}(v^{\prime})  =  \bigl( - \max_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z) }  L_{d+1}(v^{\prime}) \bigr) + d - d^{\prime} - 1
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  For the first part, observes that a vertex that is minimizing $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-} z$ is a root of unsatisfied check.  Since $\phi$-transpose sends checks to checks, we have that the check $\partial^{-} \rotfv{v^{-1}}{S/S^{\prime}} = \phi(\partial^{+}\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}})$ is not satisfied in $\partial^{-}z$ if and only if $\partial^{+}\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}}$ is not satisfied in $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. Thus, 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \min_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{-}z }L_{g}(v^{\prime}) &= \min \bigl\{ L_{g}( v^{-1} ) \colon \rotfvs{} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} \\
           &= - \max \bigl\{ L_{g}( v ) \colon \rotfvs{} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} \\
          & \ge - \max \bigl\{ L_{g}( u ) \colon  u \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}

      For the second part, observes that if $\rotfs{}$ is unsatisfied check in $\partial^{-}z$ then the vertex which gets the minimal value among the vertices on $\rotfs{ }$ is the vertex that is the farest from $v$. Namely the vertex: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    u = \bigl(\prod_{g \in S^{\prime}}g\bigr) v
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
So, similarly to the first part, and combing that the checks induce $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$ are at dimension $d^{\prime}+1$  we have: 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \min_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{-}z }L_{d+1}(v^{\prime}) &= \min \bigl\{ L_{d+1}( \bigl( \prod_{g \in S/S^{\prime}} g \bigr) v^{-1} ) \colon \rotfvs{} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} \\
          &=  \bigl(d-d^{\prime} - 1 \bigr) - \max \bigl\{ L_{d+1}( v ) \colon \rotfvs{} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} \\
          & = \bigl(d-d^{\prime} - 1 \bigr)  - \max \bigl\{ L_{g}( u ) \colon  u \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
Where the last transition is an equality since the maximum of $L_{d+1}$ is achieved at a root vertex.
\end{proof}
\inb{\textbf{Rewrite.}} \inb{\textbf{Consider removing, that is because when considering reduced forms there is also equality for $L_{g}$.}} Clearly, the equality regarding $L_{d+1}$ means that advancing 'down' a minimal vertex in $\partial^{-}z$ implies advancing 'up' a corresponding maximal vertex in $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. The following claim states that the maximum value of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$ can only decrease (the error doesn't spread in the $g$-direction) by the application of the sweep-rule in the dual picture ($\partial^{-}z$).
\begin{claim}
  For $g \in S$ denote $M = \min_{v \in \partial^{+}\phi(z) } L_{g}(v)$, and denote by $\xi$ the result of the sweep-rule over $z$. Then $\min_{v \in \partial^{+}\phi(\xi) } L_{g}(v) \le M$. 
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  Assume through contradiction that $\min_{v \in \partial^{+}\phi(\xi)} L_{g}(v) \le M$. Thus, there has to be a check that is unsatisfied in $\phi(\xi)$ and satisfied in $\phi(z)$. Denote it by $\rotfs{}$, and from the inclusion/exclusion relation, it follows that $\rotfv{v^{-1}}{ S/S^{\prime} } \in \xi$ while $\rotfv{v^{-1}}{ S/S^{\prime} } \notin z$. Thus, one of the qubits adjacent to $\rotfv{v^{-1}}{ S/S^{\prime} }$ was flipped by the sweep-rule. Denote the vertex that flipped the qubits, while performing the sweep-rule, by $w$ (if there is more than one pick arbitrary) and the vertex with the maximal $L_{g}$ value among the vertices of $\rotfs{}$ by $u$. 

  For the sake of the proof, we will say that $g^{-1}$ is on the path from vertex $w$ to vertex $v^{-1}$, if one has to go trough an edge corresponding to generator $g$ (in the opposite direction) for getting to $v^{-1}$ from $w$. There are two options, let's splitting on whether $g^{-1}$ is on the path from $w$ to $v^{-1}$. 
  
In the first case, it must be that $L_{g}(w) = \min L_{g}(v^{-1})$, and in addition, $g \notin S/S^{\prime}$, so
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    L_{g}(u) = L_{g}(v) = - L_{g}(v^{-1}) = - ( L_{g}(w) - 1 ) \le M 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
In the second case, where $g^{-1}$ is not on the path from $w$ to $v^{-1}$, it follows that $L_{g}(w) = L_{g}(v^{-1})$. Denote the flipped face by $f$. By the definition of the sweep-rule, there has to be at least one check in the negative local view of $w$ such that it's contained in $f$, and it's unsatisfied in $\partial^{-}(z)$ (Otherwise, $w$ would not flip $f$). Denote it by $\rotf{x}{J}$, and since $g^{-1}$ is not on the path between $w$ and $v^{-1}$, then it's also not on the paths from $w$ to $x$ and from $x$ to $v^{-1}$, namely $L_{g}(x) = L_{g}(w)$ and $g \notin J$. On the other hand, $\rotf{x^{-1}}{S/J} \in \partial^{+}\phi(z)$, and $g \in S/J$, so we get that the maximal value of $L_{g}$ in $\rotf{x^{-1}}{S/J}$ is $1-L_{g}(x) = 1 -L_{g}(v^{-1}) \ge L_{g}(u)$, in contradiction to the fact that $u$ exhibits $L_{g}$ value greater then $M$ .
\end{proof}


\inb{\textbf{Rewrite.}} Combining the relation of extremis of an error to its extremis in the dual image, with the argument that the sweep-rule shrinks $X$-error ($\partial^{-}$-boundaries), we infer that a full sweep-cycle shrinks an error composed of both $X$- and $Z$-components.
\begin{corollary}
\label{claim:polesmove}  
Consider an error whose $X$- and $Z$-product components lie at positions corresponding to vectors $z_{X}, z_{Z} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$. Denote by $\xi_{X}, \xi_{Z} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$ the $X, Z$ positions of the result after the application of a full sweep-cycle. Then: 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For any $i \in [d]$: 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
      \max \big\{ L_{i}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} z_{X} \cup \partial^{+}z_{Z} \big\} = \max \big\{ L_{i}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} \xi_{X} \cup  \partial^{+} \xi_{Z} \big\}  
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
    \item And for $i = d+1$ : 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
      \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(v)  : v \in \partial^{-} z_{X}  \cup \partial^{+}z_{Z} \big\} > \max \big\{  L_{d+1}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} \xi_{X} \cup \partial^{+} \xi_{Z}  \big\} + 1
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
  \end{enumerate}

\end{corollary}


\subsubsection{Ideal decoder}
Although the main role of the section is to show that a single sweep-cycle keeps error clusters under control during a noisy run (by admitting a modest shrinking), an immediate corollary of this is that the application of non-noisy multiple iterations of the sweep-cycle has the potential to erase errors, namely a decoder. Here, a classical measurement is given, we act on classical bits, and the sweep-cycle consists only of the first stage that works over the computational basis, without moving to the dual code. We formulate the corollary as follows:
\begin{corollary}
  \label{col:idealcorrection} 
  Consider the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code, induced by a Toric with side length $n$. Given an error $z \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$ on that Toric, with $\Size{z} \le n-1$. Then an application of $\Size{z}$ sweep cycles (partial cycles consisting only of the first stage) erases the error.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Notice that since $\Size{z}\le n-1$, we have that for any $g \in S$:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \max_{v \in z }L_{g}(v) -  \min_{v \in z }L_{g}(v) \le \Size{z} \le n-1
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
Thus, $z$ doesn't spread on a non-trivial cycle and therefore translates to (a collection of) finite error in the $\infty$-extension. Denote by $\xi_{t}$ the corresponding error on the infinite plane after $t$ sweep-cycles, and let $\xi_{0}$ be the corresponding error on the infinite plane prior to the first cycle. Since $\xi_{0}$ is finite, we use \Cref{claim:sweepshrink} and have that each sweep-cycle decreases the boundary by at least one, namely:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \Size{\partial^{-}\xi_{t+1}} \le \Size{\partial^{-}\xi_{t}} + 1
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  Therefore, after at most $\Size{\partial^{-} z} \le \Size{z}$ iterations, the boundary of the error vanishes. In particular, the syndrome vanishes. It remains to prove that during the decoding, no logical codewords were turned on. Denote by $\tilde{\xi}_{t}$ the correction that was applied at cycle $t$. From \Cref{claim:edge_pole}, it follows immediately that for any generator $g \in S$, it holds that:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \max \bigl\{ L_{g}(v) : v \in  \xi_{0}  \bigcup_{t} \tilde{\xi}_{t} \bigr\} \le  \max \bigl\{ L_{g}(v) : v \in  \xi_{0} \bigr\} 
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  And since the sweep-rule defines that vertices flips only bits which they are in their positive local view, we also have: 
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \max \bigl\{ L_{d+1}(v) : v \in  \xi_{0}  \bigcup_{t} \tilde{\xi}_{t} \bigr\} \le  \max \bigl\{ L_{d+1}(v) : v \in  \xi_{0} \bigr\} 
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
Thus, the support of all the qubits that were touched during the decoding, combined with the support of the initial error, satisfy:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \Size{ \bigl\{ v \colon v \in  \xi_{0}  \bigcup_{t}\tilde{\xi}_{t} \bigr\} }  & \le \Size{ \bigl\{ v \colon v \in  \xi_{0}  \bigr\} } \\ 
      & \le \Size{ z } \le n-1 
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}
