\subsection{Shrinking}
\label{subsection:shrink}
%We are going to show that the SWEEP decoder 'shrinks' the error by proving that the edges/poles of error clusters get closer as a result of a decoding step. For that, we consider the standard embedding of the grid in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and use the term of potential walls.



Errors can be thought of as assignments on the $d^{\prime}$-dimensional faces that have non-vanishing boundaries. In particular, $X$-errors can be characterized as binary assignments $z \in \mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}_{2}$ such that $\partial^{-} z \neq 0$. In this section, we assume dealing with the $\infty$-extension of a circuit (see \Cref{def:extension}), so the cubical complex is the infinite $d$-dimensional grid.

The following two claims relate the local maximum points of the potential walls $L_{1}, \ldots, L_{d}$ in an assignment $z$ to the structure of the syndrome they observe. Later, we will use that relation to infer that the decoding step of the sweep rule can't spread the error to a vertex\footnote{To faces whose vertices have higher values of $L_{1}, \ldots, L_{d}$ than the current global maximum values.} with a higher value than the current global maximum of $L_{1}, \ldots, L_{d}$. In other words, the error does not spread in the positive directions.

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:localmax}
Let $z$ be an assignment, and recall that the vertices in $\partial^{-} z$ are the set of vertices adjacent to unsatisfied checks induced by $z$. Let $v$ be a vertex in $\partial^{-} z$, and let $g$ be a generator in $S$ such that $v$ is a local maximum of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-} z$. Then $gv \notin \partial^{-} z$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  Suppose $gv \in \partial^{-} z$, then 
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      L_{g}(gv) = (gv)_{g} = 1 + v_{g} = 1 + L_{g}(v)
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
In contradiction to $v$ being a local maximum of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-} z$.
\end{proof}
\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:edge_pole}
Let $z$ be an assignment, let $v$ be a vertex in $\partial^{-} z$, and let $g$ be a generator in $S$ such that $v$ is a local maximum of $L_{g}$. Then, for any check associated with a $d^{\prime}-1$ face rooted at $v$ that contains $gv$, it is satisfied.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Assume, through contradiction, the existence of an unsatisfied check associated with a $d^{\prime}-1$ positive face rooted at $v$, which contains $gv$. This implies $gv \in \partial^{-} z$, contradicting \Cref{claim:localmax}.
\end{proof}

The $(d+1)$th potential wall $L_{d+1}$ gets is maximum on the 'corner' vertex of the error, and therefore we would like to have a stronger claim stating that its maximum value decreases. Thereby implying that the corner 'advances' and the error shrinks. For this, we start by showing that any error is equivalent, up to stabilizer addition, to an error whose 'corner' vertex - the maximal point of $L_{d+1}$ - sees a syndrome. 

We present two codes that describe local views. They are the \textit{stabilizers code at vertex} $v$ and the \textit{small code at vertex} $v$. The first corresponds to stabilizers rooted at the vertex, while the second is the collection of local views on its positive faces that satisfy its positive checks. Then, in \Cref{claim:agreement}, we consider a vertex whose $X$-checks don't admit a syndrome, meaning that its local view is in its \textit{small code}, and show that there is a codeword of its \textit{stabilizers code} that agrees with its positive faces. Addition by that stabilizer yields a new assignment excluding $v$. 

If $v$ was a unique maximum of $L_{d+1}$, then the vertices on the yielded assignments have a lower value of $L_{d+1}$. Therefore, one can assume that for a given error, the vertex that is the maximal point of $L_{d+1}$ sees a syndrome.

\begin{definition}[Stabilizers Code at Vertex $v$]
We define \textit{stabilizer code at vertex} $v$, denoted by $\text{Stab}_{v}$, as the restriction of the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code to the $d^{\prime}$-dimensional faces such that their vertices are at a distance of at most one positive step in each direction from $v$. That is equivalent to the union of all the positive $d^{\prime}$-dimensional faces of $v$ with the $d$-dimensional faces of each of its sibling $gv$, excluding faces containing $g$ as their generator. Namely:
  \begin{align*}
    \text{Stab}_{v} \colonequals  \bigl\{ z : \partial^{-} z  &= 0 \text{ and } \\
    & z \in \text{Span} \big(  \big\{  \rotfvs{} : S^{\prime} \subset S, |S^{\prime}| = d^{\prime}  \big\} \\
   & \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \  \bigcup_{g \in S} \big\{ \rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime}/g} : S^{\prime} \subset S/g , |S^{\prime}|=d^{\prime} \big\} \big) \bigr\}
  \end{align*}
\end{definition}
The checks of the code, are of the following three types: 
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Checks that are rooted at $v$, each identified by choosing $d-1$ generators from $S$. Namely: 
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \big\{ \rotfvs{}: S^{\prime} \subset S , |S^{\prime}|=d^{\prime}-1  \big\} 
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
    For each check, the bits that connect to it, are given by restricting the coboundary of the check to bits domain of $\text{Stab}_{v}$:  
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \big( \partial^{+} \rotfvs{} \big)|_{\text{Stab}_{v}} & =  \big(   \sum_{g \in S/S^{\prime}} \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime} \cup g} + \sum_{g \in S^{\prime}} \rotfv{g^{-1}v}{S^{\prime}\cup g}  \big)|_{\text{Stab}_{v}} =     \sum_{g \in S/S^{\prime}} \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime} \cup g} 
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
  \item Checks are rooted at the positive sibling of $v$. Consider such a sibling, let's say $gv$ (namely the vertex that is given by stepping in the direction $g \in S$ from $v$). Notice that any face rooted at $gv$ that contains $ggv$ cannot be contained in faces rooted at $v$. Thus, the checks of $gv$ correspond to choosing subsets from $S/g$. Namely:
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
      \bigcup_{g \in S}\big\{ \rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime}}: S^{\prime} \subset S/g , |S^{\prime}|=d^{\prime}-1  \big\}
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
    And each check is supported by:
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \big( \partial^{+} \rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime}} \big)|_{\text{Stab}_{v}} & = 
        \big(   \sum_{g^{\prime} \in S/S^{\prime}} \rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime} \cup g^{\prime}} + \sum_{g^{\prime} \in S^{\prime}} \rotfv{g^{\prime -1}gv}{S^{\prime}\cup g^{\prime}} \big)|_{\text{Stab}_{v}} \\
        &  =    \bigl(\sum_{ \substack{ g^{\prime} \in S/S^{\prime} \\ g^{\prime } \neq g }} \rotfv{g v}{S^{\prime} \cup g^{\prime}} \bigr) + \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}\cup g}
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
  \item Checks that their roots are at a two-step distance from $v$, namely vertices of the form $g^{\prime}gv$ where $g, g^{\prime} \in S$, and $g \neq g^{\prime}$. By the arguments presented in the second type, the checks are the $d^{\prime}-1$ faces which do not have the generators on the path leading from $v$ to them, i.e., $g, g^{\prime}$, in their generator set.
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \bigcup_{g,g^{\prime} in S^{\prime} }\big\{ \rotfv{g^{\prime}gv}{S^{\prime}}: S^{\prime} \subset S/\{ g, g^{\prime} \} , |S^{\prime}|=d^{\prime}-1  \big\}
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
  And for each check, its support is:
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \big( \partial^{+} \rotfv{g^{\prime}gv}{S^{\prime}}  \big)|_{\text{Stab}_{v}} & =  \big(    \sum_{g^{\prime \prime} \in S/S^{\prime}} \rotfv{gg^{\prime}v}{S^{\prime} \cup g^{\prime \prime}} + \sum_{g \in S^{\prime}} \rotfv{g^{\prime \prime -1}gg^{\prime}v}{S^{\prime}\cup g^{\prime \prime}} \big)|_{\text{Stab}_{v}}\\
        &=    \rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime} \cup g^{\prime}} + \rotfv{g^{\prime}v}{S^{\prime}\cup g}   
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Small Code at Vertex $v$]
  For a vertex $v$, we denote by $\mathcal{C}_{v}$ the \textit{small code at vertex $v$}, which is defined to be the binary assignments over the bits ($d^{\prime}$ faces) rooted at $v$ that satisfy the positive checks of $v$, namely:   
  \begin{align*}
    \mathcal{C}_{v} \colonequals  \bigl\{ z : (\partial^{-} z)|_{v+}  &= 0 \text{ and } \\
    & z \in \text{Span} \big(  \big\{  \rotfvs{} : S^{\prime} \subset S, |S^{\prime}| = d^{\prime}  \big\} \big) \bigr\} 
  \end{align*}
The checks of the code are exactly the first type checks of $\text{Stab}_{v}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{claim}

\end{claim}

\begin{conjecture} 
  \label{conj:agreement}
For any integers $d$ and $d^{\prime} < d$, $G_{\mathcal{C}_{v}}$ is contained in $G_{\text{Stab}_{v}}$. In particular, \Cref{claim:agreement} holds for any dimension.

Now we are ready to prove that any error is equivalent, up to addition by stabilizer, to an error whose maximal $L_{d+1}$ vertex (or its edge) sees a syndrome (and that maximum is equal to or lower than the maximal value of $L_{d+1}$ in the original error). \Cref{claim:reduce_stab} introduces a 'single application' of \Cref{claim:agreement} to 'advance' the corner (the vertex that gets the maximal value of $L_{d+1}$). The corner might not be unique, so in the proof, we advance all the corners simultaneously. Yet, it might occur that the yielded assignment still doesn't admit corners that see a syndrome. Thus, \Cref{claim:extermum} repeats  'corners advancing' steps on until it ensures the desired outcome - that the corner vertex of the final yielded assignment sees a syndrome. 
\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:reduce_stab}
  Let $z$ be a finite assignment. Suppose that 
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
            \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in z \big\} > \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} z \big\}
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  Then there is $\tilde{z} \sim_{\mathcal{C}_{X}} z$ such that:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \max \left\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \tilde{z} \right\} & \le  \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in z \big\} - 1
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  Let $v_{1}, v_{2}, \ldots, v_{k}$ be the vertices in $z$ that achieve the maximum for $L_{d+1}$, namely $v_{1}, v_{2}, \ldots, v_{k} = \arg \max_{v \in z} L_{d+1}(v)$, and assume that $v_{i} \notin \partial^{-} z$ for any $i \in [k]$. (Notice that the maximum is indeed achieved since $z$ is finite.)

Consider the $i$th vertex $v_{i}$. By definition, since $v_{i} \notin \partial^{-} z$, we have $\partial^{-} z|_{v_{i}} = 0$, and since $v_{i}$ is a maximum point of $L_{d+1}$ in $z$, it is also a local maximum, and therefore $z|_{v_{i}+} = z|_{v_{i}}$. 
Combining the two, we get that $z|_{v_{i}}$ is a codeword of the small code at $v_{i}$, namely $z|_{v_{i}} \in \mathcal{C}_{v_{i}}$. Thus, by \Cref{claim:agreement}, there is a stabilizer $w_{i} \in \text{Stab}_{v_{i}}$ such that $w_{i}$ and $z$ agree on the positive faces of $v_{i}$, namely $w_{i}|_{v_{i}} = z|_{v_{i}}$. 

Set $\tilde{z} \leftarrow z + \sum_{i}w_{i}$. Since $\sum_{i}w_{i}$ is a stabilizer, we have $\tilde{z} \sim_{C_{X}} z$. In addition, for any $j \neq i$, it follows that $v_{j} \notin w_{i}$.
Otherwise, there exists $S^{\prime} \subset S$ such that $v_{j} \in \rotf{v_{i}}{S^{\prime}}/ v_{i}$, and therefore $L_{d+1}(v_{j}) > L_{d+1}(v_{i})$, in contradiction to $v_{i}$ and $v_{j}$ being both maximum points for $L_{d+1}$ in $\partial^{-} z$. 
Thus, we have:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \tilde{z}|_{v_{i}} = (z + \sum_{j}w_{j})|_{v_{i}} =  (z + w_{i})|_{v_{i}} = 0
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Namely, for any $i$, the $i$th vertex $v_{i}$ is not in $\tilde{z}$. Hence, the vertex domain of $\tilde{z}$ is contained in the union of the vertices of $z$ with the vertices of $w_{1}, w_{2}, \ldots, w_{k}$, substituting $v_{1}, v_{2}, \ldots, v_{k}$. Overall, we get:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
  \max \left\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \tilde{z} \right\} & \le \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in z \bigcup_{i} w_{i} \big/ \bigcup_{i} v_{i} \big\}  \\ 
     & \le  \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in z \big\} - 1
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:extermum}
  Let $z$ be a finite assignment. Then there is $\tilde{z} \sim_{\mathcal{C}_{X}} z$ such that 
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in \tilde{z} \big\} = \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} z \big\}
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Let $z$ be a finite assignment, and let $k$ be an integer such that $k \ge 1$ and
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
            \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in z \big\} =  \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} z \big\} + k
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  For an integer $l \le k$, consider the assignments $\tilde{z}_{0},\tilde{z}_{1},..,\tilde{z}_{l}$ defined as follow: 
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The first element, $\tilde{z}_{0} = z$. 
    \item If the $i-1$th element satisfies 
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      (\star ) \ \ \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in \tilde{z}_{i-1} \big\} > \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} \tilde{z}_{i-1} \big\} 
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
      Then define the $i$th element, $\tilde{z}_{i}$, to be the result of applying the construction, given in \Cref{claim:reduce_stab}, on $\tilde{z}_{i-1}$ , namely  $\partial \tilde{z}_{i+1} = \partial z_{i}$ and  
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in \tilde{z}_{i} \big\} \le  \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \tilde{z}_{i-1} \big\} - 1
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
If the $i-1$th element doesn't satisfies $\star$, then set $l = i -1$.
\item If the $k$th element was defined, set $l = k$.
  \end{itemize}
Since $\sim_{C_{X}}$ is a transitive relation, we have that for any $i \in [l]$, $z \sim_{C_{X}} \tilde{z}_{i}$. If $l \neq k$, then there exists $\tilde{z}_{i}$ such that $\tilde{z}_{i} \sim_{C_{X}}$ and $\tilde{z}_{i}$ satisfies $\star$, namely by setting $\tilde{z} = \tilde{z}_{i}$, we get the desired.
So, it is left to handle the case where $l = k$. In that case, set $\tilde{z} = \tilde{z}_{k}$. By \Cref{claim:reduce_stab}:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in \tilde{z} \big\} \le  \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} z \big\}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Yet, since $\tilde{z} \sim_{C_{X}} z$, it follows that $\partial \tilde{z} = \partial z$, So: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
     \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in \partial^{-} z \big\} = \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in  \partial^{-} \tilde{z} \big\} \le \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in z \big\} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Therefore, we have the equality:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in \tilde{z} \big\} = \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} z \big\}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}



Next, we prove that the sweep-cycle advances the corner of the error's boundary. We assume we are given an error whose corner sees a syndrome, and thereby it is also the corner of the boundary, and show that the sweep-rule application excludes the corner. In other words, the sweep-rule shrinks $X$-errors. 
\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:sweepshrink}
Let $z$ be a finite assignment and let $\xi$ be the result of the application of the sweep rule on it. Then: 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For any $i \in [d]$: $\max \big\{ L_{i}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} z \big\} = \max \big\{ L_{i}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} \xi \big\}  $
    \item And for $i = d+1$ : $ \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(v)  : v \in \partial^{-} z \big\} > \max \big\{  L_{d+1}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} \xi \big\} + 1$
  \end{enumerate}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
First, since the sweep rule is invariant to addition by stabilizers, for any $\tilde{z}$ such that $\tilde{z} \sim_{C_{X}} z$, the correction applied by the sweep rule on $\tilde{z}$ is the same as the application on $z$. Denote by $\phi$ the applied correction, by $\tilde{\xi}$ the result of applying the sweep rule on $\tilde{z}$, and by $w$ the stabilizer which is the difference between $z$ and $\tilde{z}$, namely:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \xi &= z + \phi \\
      \tilde{\xi} &= \tilde{z} + \phi \\
      \tilde{z} &= z + w
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  The $\partial^{-}$ boundary of $\tilde{\xi}$ equals:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \partial^{-} \tilde{\xi} = \partial^{-} \left( \tilde{z} + \phi \right) = \partial^{-} \left( z + \phi \right) = \partial^{-} \xi
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  So, in total, we have that for any $\tilde{z}$ such that $\tilde{z} \sim_{C_{X}} z$, it holds that $\partial^{-} \tilde{z} = \partial^{-} z$ and $\partial^{-} \tilde{\xi} = \partial^{-} \xi$. Therefore, showing that there exists $\tilde{z} \sim_{C_{X}} z$ for which the claim holds proves the claim for $z$. By \Cref{claim:extermum}, there is $\tilde{z} \sim_{C_{X}} z$ such that:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) :  u \in \tilde{z} \big\} = \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} \tilde{z} \big\}
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  
  For such $\tilde{z}$, any maximum of $L_{d+1}$ in $ \partial^{-} \tilde{z} $, is also a maximum in $\tilde{z}$. Denote by $v_{1},..,v_{k}$ the maximum points in $\partial^{-} \tilde{z}$. Since they are maximum points in $\tilde{z}$, it holds that for each $i \in [k]$, $\tilde{z}|_{v_{i}} = \tilde{z}|_{v_{i}+}$, and hence $\partial^{-} \tilde{z}|_{v_{i}} = \partial^{-} \tilde{z}|_{v_{i}+}$. Since $\tilde{z}|_{v_{i}+}$ is an assignment rooted at $v_{i}$, then $v_{i}$ can suggest $\tilde{z}|_{v_{i} +}$, namely the condition in sweep rule is satisfied for vertex $v_{i}$. Thus:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
\partial^{-}\tilde{\xi}|_{v_{i}} =  \partial^{-}\tilde{z}|_{v_{i}} + \partial^{-}\phi|_{v_{i}} = 0
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Namely, for any $i$, the $i$th vertex $v_{i}$ is not in $\tilde{\xi}$. For any vertex $v$ denote by $\phi_{v}$ the correction made by the vertex $v$, namely $\phi = \sum_{v \in \partial^{-} \tilde{z}} \phi_{v}$. By the same arguments as in the proof of \Cref{claim:reduce_stab}, we have that $v_{j} \notin \phi_{v_{i}}$. Hence, the vertex domain of $\tilde{\xi}$ is contained in the union of the vertices of $\tilde{z}$ with the vertices of $ \cup_{v \in V} \phi_{v} $, substituting maximum points $v_{1}, v_{2}, \ldots, v_{k}$. Overall, we get:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \max \left\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} \tilde{\xi} \right\} & \le \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in  \partial^{-} \tilde{z} \bigcup_{v \in \partial^{-} \tilde{z} } \phi_{v} \big/ \bigcup_{i} v_{i} \big\}  \\ 
    & \le  \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} \tilde{z} \big\} - 1
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
And we proved the second part of the claim. It's left to prove the first. Let $v$ be a vertex that is a maximum of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-} \tilde{z}$. Maximum points of $L_{g}$ do not see syndrome at the $g$ direction. To see why, denote the applied correction at $v$ by: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \phi_{v} &= \sum_{S^{\prime}_{i}} \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}_{i}} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
By \Cref{claim:edge_pole} we have that $\partial^{-} \tilde{z}|_{gv} = 0 $, Hence: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \partial^{-} \phi_{v}|_{gv} &= \left( \partial^{-} \sum_{S^{\prime}_{i}} \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}_{i}} \right)|_{gv} \\
    &=  \left(  \sum_{S^{\prime}_{i}} \sum_{g^{\prime} \in S^{\prime}_{i} }\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}_{i}/g^{\prime}} +\rotfv{g^{\prime}v}{S^{\prime}_{i}/g^{\prime}} \right)|_{gv} \\ 
  & = 0 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Thus if  $g  \in \cup_{i} S^{\prime}_{i}$, then there is $S^{\prime}_{i}$ such: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime}_{i}/g}  \in \partial^{-} \phi_{v}|_{gv} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
So, to satisfy $\partial^{-} \tilde{z}|_{gv} = 0$, the term $\rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime}_{i}/g}$ must appear at least twice in the sum. Yet, the left terms in the sum cannot contain it (they differ by the root vertex), and if there is a right term $\rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime}_{j}/g}$ such that $\rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime}_{j}/g} = \rotfv{gv}{S^{\prime}_{i}/g}$, then it implies that $S_{j} = S_{i}$, which is a contradiction. Therefore, $g \notin \cup S^{\prime}_{i}$ and $gv \notin \phi_{v}$. 


Overall, we find that the vertex domain of $\partial^{-}\tilde{\xi}$ does not contain $gv$ for any $v$ that is a maximum of $L_{g}$. Thus, the value of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-}\tilde{\xi}$ doesn't increase, namely:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \max \left\{ L_{g}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} \tilde{\xi} \right\} & \le  \max \big\{ L_{g}(u) : u \in \partial^{-} \tilde{z} \big\} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}
We finished analyzing the first part of the sweep-cycle that acts in the computational basis. In the next section, we show that the second part of the cycle shrinks phase errors.
\subsubsection{The Sweep-rule Phase In The Dual Code}
Now, we will think of the binary assignment $z \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$ as the positions of the $Z$-errors, after permuting the qubits by the $\phi$-transpose. Namely, $\partial^{-}z$ is the assignment boundary that we would like to shrink, and $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$ is its source prior to the reordering. 

In this stage of the sweep-cycle, the sweep-rule acts in opposite directions. 
%We will keep $S$ for denoting the 'current' positive directions relative
Thus, it will be sufficient to show that the extremities of the potential walls in $\partial^{-} z$ mirror the extremities of the potential walls of $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. Subsequently, since the sweep-rule shrinks $\partial^{-}z$ (with sign adjustments), we have that the $\phi$-transpose of the yielded assignment also admits a shrunk coboundary relative to $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. 


However, we would not be able to show an exact mirroring, yet we show that the situation is close, and that would be enough. Specifically, we will show that for $g \in S$, any of the extremities of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-} z$ are higher (sent closer) than the mirrored extremities of $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. For $L_{d+1}$, the extremities of $\partial^{-}z$ have an equal $L_{d+1}$ value to the mirrored extremities in $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$ plus $d - d^{\prime} - 1$.

\label{subsection:dualcode}
\begin{claim}
Let $z$ be an assignment. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For any generator $g \in S$, and potential wall $L_{g}$ it holds that: 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \min_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{-}z }L_{g}(v^{\prime}) \ge  - \max_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z) }  L_{g}(v^{\prime}) 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
     \item The following equality holds:
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \max_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{-}z }L_{d+1}(v^{\prime})  =  \bigl( - \max_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z) }  L_{d+1}(v^{\prime}) \bigr) + d - d^{\prime} - 1
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  For the first part, observes that a vertex that is minimizing $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{-} z$ is a root of unsatisfied check.  Since $\phi$-transpose sends checks to checks, we have that the check $\partial^{-} \rotfv{v^{-1}}{S/S^{\prime}} = \phi(\partial^{+}\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}})$ is not satisfied in $\partial^{-}z$ if and only if $\partial^{+}\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}}$ is not satisfied in $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. Thus, 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \min_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{-}z }L_{g}(v^{\prime}) &= \min \bigl\{ L_{g}( v^{-1} ) \colon \rotfvs{} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} \\
           &= - \max \bigl\{ L_{g}( v ) \colon \rotfvs{} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} \\
          & \ge - \max \bigl\{ L_{g}( u ) \colon  u \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}

      For the second part, observes that if $\rotfs{}$ is unsatisfied check in $\partial^{-}z$ then the vertex which gets the minimal value among the vertices on $\rotfs{ }$ is the vertex that is the farest from $v$. Namely the vertex: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    u = \bigl(\prod_{g \in S^{\prime}}g\bigr) v
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
So, similarly to the first part, and combing that the checks induce $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$ are at dimension $d^{\prime}+1$  we have: 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \min_{v^{\prime} \in \partial^{-}z }L_{d+1}(v^{\prime}) &= \min \bigl\{ L_{d+1}( \bigl( \prod_{g \in S/S^{\prime}} g \bigr) v^{-1} ) \colon \rotfvs{} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} \\
          &=  \bigl(d-d^{\prime} - 1 \bigr) - \max \bigl\{ L_{d+1}( v ) \colon \rotfvs{} \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} \\
          & = \bigl(d-d^{\prime} - 1 \bigr)  - \max \bigl\{ L_{g}( u ) \colon  u \in \partial^{+} \phi(z)  \bigr\} 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
Where the last transition is an equality since the maximum of $L_{d+1}$ is achieved at a root vertex.
\end{proof}
Clearly, the equality regarding $L_{d+1}$ means that advancing 'down' a minimal vertex in $\partial^{-}z$ implies advancing 'up' a corresponding maximal vertex in $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$. The following claim states that the maximum value of $L_{g}$ in $\partial^{+}\phi(z)$ can only decrease (the error doesn't spread in the $g$-direction) by the application of the sweep-rule in the dual picture ($\partial^{-}z$).
\begin{claim}
  For $g \in S$ denote $M = \min_{v \in \partial^{+}\phi(z) } L_{g}(v)$, and denote by $\xi$ the result of the sweep-rule over $z$. Then $\min_{v \in \partial^{+}\phi(\xi) } L_{g}(v) \le M$. 
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  Assume through contradiction that $\min_{v \in \partial^{+}\phi(\xi)} L_{g}(v) \le M$. Thus, there has to be a check that is unsatisfied in $\phi(\xi)$ and satisfied in $\phi(z)$. Denote it by $\rotfs{}$, and from the inclusion/exclusion relation, it follows that $\rotfv{v^{-1}}{ S/S^{\prime} } \in \xi$ while $\rotfv{v^{-1}}{ S/S^{\prime} } \notin z$. Thus, one of the qubits adjacent to $\rotfv{v^{-1}}{ S/S^{\prime} }$ was flipped by the sweep-rule. Denote the vertex that flipped the qubits, while performing the sweep-rule, by $w$ (if there is more than one pick arbitrary) and the vertex with the maximal $L_{g}$ value among the vertices of $\rotfs{}$ by $u$. 

  For the sake of the proof, we will say that $g^{-1}$ is on the path from vertex $w$ to vertex $v^{-1}$, if one has to go trough an edge corresponding to generator $g$ (in the opposite direction) for getting to $v^{-1}$ from $w$. There are two options, let's splitting on whether $g^{-1}$ is on the path from $w$ to $v^{-1}$. 
  
In the first case, it must be that $L_{g}(w) = \min L_{g}(v^{-1})$, and in addition, $g \notin S/S^{\prime}$, so
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    L_{g}(u) = L_{g}(v) = - L_{g}(v^{-1}) = - ( L_{g}(w) - 1 ) \le M 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
In the second case, where $g^{-1}$ is not on the path from $w$ to $v^{-1}$, it follows that $L_{g}(w) = L_{g}(v^{-1})$. Denote the flipped face by $f$. By the definition of the sweep-rule, there has to be at least one check in the negative local view of $w$ such that it's contained in $f$, and it's unsatisfied in $\partial^{-}(z)$ (Otherwise, $w$ would not flip $f$). Denote it by $\rotf{x}{J}$, and since $g^{-1}$ is not on the path between $w$ and $v^{-1}$, then it's also not on the paths from $w$ to $x$ and from $x$ to $v^{-1}$, namely $L_{g}(x) = L_{g}(w)$ and $g \notin J$. On the other hand, $\rotf{x^{-1}}{S/J} \in \partial^{+}\phi(z)$, and $g \in S/J$, so we get that the maximal value of $L_{g}$ in $\rotf{x^{-1}}{S/J}$ is $1-L_{g}(x) = 1 -L_{g}(v^{-1}) \ge L_{g}(u)$, in contradiction to the fact that $u$ exhibits $L_{g}$ value greater then $M$ .
\end{proof}


Combining the relation of extremis of an error to its extremis in the dual image, with the argument that the sweep-rule shrinks $X$-error ($\partial^{-}$-boundaries), we infer that a full sweep-cycle shrinks an error composed of both $X$- and $Z$-components.
\begin{corollary}
\label{claim:polesmove}  
Consider an error whose $X$- and $Z$-product components lie at positions corresponding to vectors $z_{X}, z_{Z} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$. Denote by $\xi_{X}, \xi_{Z} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$ the $X, Z$ positions of the result after the application of a full sweep-cycle. Then: 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item For any $i \in [d]$: 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
      \max \big\{ L_{i}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} z_{X} \cup \partial^{+}z_{Z} \big\} = \max \big\{ L_{i}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} \xi_{X} \cup  \partial^{+} \xi_{Z} \big\}  
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
    \item And for $i = d+1$ : 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
      \max \big\{ L_{d+1}(v)  : v \in \partial^{-} z_{X}  \cup \partial^{+}z_{Z} \big\} > \max \big\{  L_{d+1}(v) : v \in \partial^{-} \xi_{X} \cup \partial^{+} \xi_{Z}  \big\} + 1
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
  \end{enumerate}

\end{corollary}


\subsubsection{Ideal decoder}
Although the main role of the section is to show that a single sweep-cycle keeps error clusters under control during a noisy run (by admitting a modest shrinking), an immediate corollary of this is that the application of non-noisy multiple iterations of the sweep-cycle has the potential to erase errors, namely a decoder. That gives us the following corollary:
\begin{corollary}
  \label{col:idealcorrection} 
  Consider the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code, induced by a Toric with side length $n$. Given an error $z \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$ on that Toric, with $\Size{z} \le \frac{n}{2}$. Then an application of $\Size{z}$ sweep cycles erases the error.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Notice that since $\Size{z}\le {n}{2}$, we have that for any $g \in S$:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \max_{v \in z }L_{g}(v) -  \min{v \in z }L_{g}(v) \le \Size{z} \le \frac{n}{2}
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
Thus, $z$ doesn't spread on a non-trivial cycle and therefore translates to (a collection of) finite error in the $\infty$-extension. Denote by $\xi_{t}$ the corresponding error on the infinite plane after $t$ sweep-cycles, and let $\xi_{0}$ be the corresponding error on the infinite plane prior to the first cycle. Since $\xi_{0}$ is finite, we use \Cref{claim:polesmove} and have that each sweep-cycle decreases the boundary by at least one, namely:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \Size{\partial^{-}\xi_{t+1}} \le \Size{\partial^{-}\xi_{t}} + 1
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
Therefore, after at most $\Size{\partial^{-} z} \le \Size{z}$ iterations, the boundary of the error vanishes. In particular, the syndrome vanishes. It remains to prove that during the decoding, no logical codewords were turned on. Denote by $\tilde{\xi}_{t}$ the correction that was applied at cycle $t$.

  
By the definition of the sweep-rule, $\Size{\partial^{-}\tilde{\xi}_{t}} = \Size{\tilde{\xi}_{t}}$ (write the proof). Thus, the support of all the qubits that were touched during the decoding, combined with the support of the initial error, satisfies:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \Size{  \xi_{0}  \bigcup_{t}\tilde{\xi}_{t} }  & \le \Size{\xi_{0}} + \sum_{t}\Size{\tilde{\xi}_{t}} \\ 
      & \le \Size{\xi_{0}} + \sum_{t}\Size{\partial^{-}\tilde{\xi}_{t}} \\ 
      & \le \Size{\xi_{0}} + \Size{\partial^{-}\xi_{0}} \le \frac{n}{2} 
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}
