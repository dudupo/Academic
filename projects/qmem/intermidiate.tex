
\subsection{The Intermediate Argument} 
An essential ingredient of our proof is an 'intermediate argument', stating that prior to having a large (reduced) error that closes a non-trivial loop on the Toric, there must be a step in which there is a (reduced) error that on one hand doesn't close a non-trivial loop, yet it has a size that is comparable to the Toric side. Before the first step of that kind, we can assume that no error closes a non-trivial loop; namely, the errors are finite. That implies the following two: first, the condition for \Cref{claim:decodestopnoise} is valid, and therefore the replacement argument can be used to infer a correctness of logical measurement gadgets. Second, the sweep rule is, in some sense, effective (\Cref{claim:polesmove}), and soon we will see that it sets a structure for the histories of slices. 

The following claim relates the size of the reduced error to the sizes of the reduced errors that compose it.
    \begin{claim}
      \label{claim:unionofred}
      Consider a $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chains $y \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$, and let $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{m} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}}$ be $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chains such that, there is a reduced form of $y$ which is a disjoint of reduced forms of $x_{1},..,x_{m}$, and that reduced form is connected.  Then: 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \Size{ \text{reduced form of } (y) } \le \sum_{i}^{m}{\Size{ \text{reduced form of } (x_{i}) } }
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
    \end{claim}
    \begin{proof}
For each $x_{i}$, denote by $z_{i}$ the stabilizer for which the translation of $x_{i}$ by $z_{i}$ yields the reduced form that satisfies the given conditions. Namely:
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          z_{i} = \arg \min_{z \in \mathcal{C}_{Z}^{\perp} }\Size{ x_{i} + z } 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
      and $\sum_{i} x_{i} + z_{i}$ is a connected reduced form of $y$. Where we used the fact that union of two disjoint chains is their sum.  Thus:
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \Size{ \text{reduced form of } {y} } & =  \min_{z \in \mathcal{C}_{Z}^{\perp} } \Size{z + y }  \le  \min_{z \in \mathcal{C}_{Z}^{\perp} }\Size{z + x_{1} + x_{2} + .. + x_{m}}\\
          & \le \Size{ (x_{1}+z_{1}) + (x_{2}+z_{2}) + .. + (x_{m} + z_{i}) } \\
          &\le \sum_{i}^{m}{ \Size{x_{i}+z_{i} } }
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
Where the last transition follows from \Cref{claim:tringinq}, combined with the connectivity condition.
    \end{proof}

Having that claim, we can prove the intermediate argument:
\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:intersyn}
  There is a constant $\alpha \in (0,1)$ depending only on $\Delta,d,d^{\prime}$ satisfies the following: Denote by $A$ the event that there is a step that admits an infinite error. In addition, for any step $\tau \in J$, denote by $B_{\tau}$ the event that step $\tau$ is the first step to admit a finite error at size at least $\alpha n$. Denote by $w$ and $\mu$ the width and the depth of $C[\mathcal{E}]$. Then, for sufficiently large $n$, the probability that $A$ occurs is bounded by:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \prb{A} \le w\mu\max_{\tau\in J }\prb{B_{\tau}}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}
  Let $\mathcal{H}_{\tau}$ and $\mathcal{H}_{\tau+1}$ be the analysis Toom's contours at steps $\tau$ and $\tau+1$. And let $P_{1}, \ldots, P_{t}, \tilde{P}_{1}$ and $P_{1}, \ldots, P_{t}, \tilde{P}_{2}$ be the Pauli-paths (up to a step) which their reduced version (reduced form of their view in $\infty$-extension) induce the Toom's contours. In addition, let $g$ be the gate that is applied at step $\tau+1$. Assume that step $\tau+1$ is the first to admit an infinite error. For the sake of the proof, let us denote by $C[\mathcal{E}]|_{\rightarrow \tau}$ the quantum circuit up to step $\tau$, so
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
 C[\mathcal{E}]|_{\rightarrow \tau+1} = g C[\mathcal{E}]|_{\rightarrow \tau}  
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
By definition of Pauli-path (\Cref{def:paulipath}), we have that:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \Trr{} \big( (C[\mathcal{E}]|_{\tau} \circ P_{t}) \tilde{P}_{1}   \big) \neq 0 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Thus, there are constants $\gamma, \bar{\gamma} \in \mathbb{R}$ and an operator $\mathbf{A} \in \mathcal{L}$, such that $\Trr{} (\mathbf{A} \tilde{P}_{1}) = 0$ and:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
  C[\mathcal{E}]|_{\tau} \circ P_{t} = \gamma \tilde{P}_{1} + \bar{\gamma} \mathbf{A}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Therefore: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    &\Trr{} \big( (C[\mathcal{E}]|_{\tau + 1} \circ P_{t}) \tilde{P}_{2}   \big) = \Trr{} \big(   (gC[\mathcal{E}]|_{\tau}  \circ P_{t}) \tilde{P}_{2}   \big) = \gamma \Trr{} \big( (g \circ \tilde{P}_{1} )  \tilde{P}_{2} \big) +  \bar{\gamma} \Trr{} \big (g \circ \mathbf{A} )  \tilde{P}_{2}   \big) 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
We argue that we can assume, without loss of generality, that the term $ \Trr{} ( (g \circ \tilde{P}_{1} )  \tilde{P}_{2} ) $ is non-zero. To see this, suppose that it vanishes; then there has to be another Pauli term $\tilde{P} \in \mathcal{P}$ in the decomposition of $\mathbf{A}$ for which:
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \Trr{} \big( (g \circ \tilde{P} )  \tilde{P}_{2} \big) \neq 0
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Otherwise, the whole trace is zeroing out in contradiction to the fact that $P_{1},..,P_{t},\tilde{P_{2}}$ is a Pauli-path. So, we could initially choose the Pauli-path that induces '$\mathcal{H}_{\tau}$'\footnote{Observe that the 'reinduced' Toom's counter is different from $\mathcal{H}_{\tau}$.} to be $P_{1},..,P_{t},\tilde{P}$.


Now, denote by $\tilde{Q}_{1}, \tilde{Q}_{2}$ the reduced forms of the images of $\tilde{P}_{1}, \tilde{P}_{2}$ in the $\infty$-extension. Since $g$ acts on at most $\Delta$ qubits, any chain $x \subset \text{supp } X(\tilde{Q}_{2})$, which among $g$'s translation supports only the trivial-translation of $g$, intersects at most constant number, which is depending on $\Delta, d^{\prime},d $ of  reduced chains in $\text{supp } X(\tilde{Q}_{1})$. Thus, we can say that $x$ is a union of at most constant number of disjoint reduced chains in $\text{supp } X(\tilde{Q}_{1})$ with at most disjoint reduced $\Delta$ chains, of a single face, which might be introduced by $g$. Denote the reduced chains in the union by $x_{1},..,x_{l}$, such $l = \alpha^{-1}$. So if $x$ is connected, then by \Cref{claim:unionofred} we have that:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \Size{x} \le \sum_{i=1}^{l} \Size{x_{i} }\le \alpha^{-1} \max_{i}\Size{x_{i}}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Since the maximal size of a reduced connected chain introduced by trivial translation of $g$ faces alone is bounded by a function of $\Delta$ and $d$, it follows that if $\Size{x} \ge n$, then there must be a reduced chain $x_{i}$ in the support of $X(\tilde{Q}_{1})$ of size at least $\alpha n$.
Thus, it remains to prove that there is a connected chain $x \in \tilde{Q}_{2}$, which does not support a non-trivial translation of $g$, and has size at least $n$. By invariance to translation, we can substitute the requirement of supporting only the non-trivial translation of $g$ by supporting at most one translation of $g$.




We pick such $x$ by the following algorithm: pick an infinite connected reduced error $x^{\prime}$ in $\text{supp } X(\tilde{Q}_{2})$. If $x^{\prime}$ supports only one translation of $g$, then pick $x=x^{\prime}$. Otherwise, if $x^{\prime}$ supports at least two translations of $g$, pick a pair of translations as follows: Let $f_{1}, f_{2}$ be faces, each supporting a different translation in that pair. We choose the pair such that the path from $f_{1}$ to $f_{2}$ through $x^{\prime}$'s edges is the shortest among any choice of the translations and the faces $f_{1}, f_{2}$ within them, where we use that $x^{\prime}$ is connected to infer that there has to be at least one path. Then, set $x$ to be the chain obtained by excluding $f_{2}$ from the path. Now, if $x$ contains a face that supports the second translation of $g$, then we get a contradiction to the minimality of the path. Thus $x$ is a connected chain that supports a single translation of $g$, and since it contains a face and its translation, then its size is at least $n$.


We got that a condition for step $\tau+1$ to admit an infinite error is that a step $\tau$ admits a error at size at least $\alpha n$. Thus, the desired inequality follows by taking the union bound:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \prb{A} \le w\mu\max_{\tau\in J}\prb{B_{\tau}}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{proof}
