

\subsection{Intermediate} 

The following definition of propagation describes the evolution of a fault. It assumes an order of steps and defines an operator that is obtained by commuting a given fault with the gates (and faults) prior to a given step.

\begin{definition}[Fault propagation to a step $\tau$]
  \label{def:localprop}
  Let $C=\{u_i\}_i$ be a quantum circuit, let $J=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)$ be an order agreeing with $\pi_C$, and let $u_i\in C$. Suppose that $u_i=u_{j_l}$ in this order. If $l>\tau$, we define the propagation of $u_i$ to step $\tau$ to be the identity operator.
Otherwise, define operators $\mathcal{D}_0,\mathcal{D}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{\tau-l}$ recursively by setting $\mathcal{D}_0=u_{j_l}$ and requiring that for every $1\le s\le \tau-l$,
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \mathcal{D}_{s-1}u_{j_{l+s}} = u_{j_{l+s}}\mathcal{D}_s.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  We then define the \emph{propagation of $u_i$ to step $\tau$} to be the operator
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \mathcal{D} \colonequals \mathcal{D}_{\tau-l}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
Now let $C[\mathcal{E}]$ be a noisy circuit, where all of its quantum registers are encoded in the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code with side length $n$. Let $J$ be an order agreeing with $\pi_{C[\mathcal{E}]}$, and let $\tau$ be a step in that order. For each faulty gate $e \in \mathcal{E}$ that occurs no later than step $\tau$, denote by $\mathcal{D}^{\tau}_{e}$ its propagation to step $\tau$. We define the propagated error at step $\tau$ by multiplying these propagated faults in reverse topological order.
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \mathcal{D}^{\tau} \colonequals \prod_{e\preceq \tau} \mathcal{D}^{\tau}_{e}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
Moreover, we say that a step $\tau$ admits an infinite $X$-error if there is a register such that in the view of the $\infty$-extension of $C[\mathcal{E}]$, the propagated error at step $\tau$, $\mathcal{D}^{\tau}$, has support on a connected, reduced, $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain $x$ such that $\Size{x} \ge n$.
\end{definition}


\inb{\textbf{Rewrite.}} The propagation up to step $\tau$ allows us to define the deviation from an ideal run by propagating all the faults and marking all the locations in the result. 


\begin{claim}
  Let $\alpha = (\Delta d^{\prime}(d-d^{\prime}+1))^{-1}$. Denote by $A$ the event that there is a step that admits an infinite $X$-error. In addition, for any step $\tau \in J$, denote by $B_{\tau}$ the event that step $\tau$ is the first step for which there is a register such that, in the view of the $\infty$-extension, $\mathcal{D}^{\tau}$ has support on a connected, reduced, $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain $x$ such that $\Size{x} = \alpha n$. Denote by $w$ and $\mu$ the width and the depth of $C[\mathcal{E}]$. Then the probability that $A$ occurs is bounded by:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \prb{A} \le w\mu\max_{\tau\in[w\mu]}\prb{B_{\tau}}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{claim}


\begin{proof}
  For the sake of the proof, we say that a chain $x$ is in operator $O$ if, when considering the Pauli decomposition of the image of $O$ in the $\infty$-extension, there is a correspondence between the faces of $x$ and the (partial) support of one of the terms in $O$'s image.

  Denote by $x$ the maximal, in size, reduced connected $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain in $\mathcal{D}^{\tau+1}$. Assume that $\tau$ is the first step to admits an infinite $X$-error, thus any chain in $\mathcal{D}^{\tau}$ has a size lower than $n$. Denote by $u$ the gates which is applied at step $\tau$, so there have to be paulis $P_{1},P_{2}$ such that: 
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      P_{1} \in \text{Supp } \mathcal{D}^{\tau}, \, \,  P_{2} \in \text{Supp } \mathcal{D}^{\tau + 1}, \, \, x \text{ is both in } P_{1},P_{2}  \, \, \text{ and } \expval*{P_{1}|u|P_{2}} \neq 0
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  Since $u$ has a fan at most $\Delta$, and since any qubit ($d^{\prime}$-dimensional face) is connected to at most $d^{\prime}(d-d^{\prime}+1)$ qubits (by stepping through a check\footnote{a $d^{\prime}-1$ dimensional face}), we get that the application of $u$ on $P_{1}$ touches at most $\alpha^{-1}$ chains, and the result of the modification yields $x$. Therefore, $x$ resides inside a union of at most $\alpha^{-1}$, and since $x$ is connected, we have that its size is bounded by summing the size of each chain in the union. Therefore, if any of the chains is at size at most $\alpha n$, it implies that the size of $x$ is lower than $n$. Hence, a necessary condition to step $\tau+1$ to admit an infinite error is that step $\tau-1$ admits errors at size greater than $\alpha n$, namely the occurrence of $B_{\tau}$. The inequality follows by using the union bound over all the steps in $C[\mathcal{E}]$.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Gottesman argument}

\begin{claim}
  Let $\gamma, \gamma^{\prime} \in (0,1)$, and denote by $n$ the side-length of the Toric. Let $x$ be the error, and assume that the probability of reduced error, time-connected, cluster $x_{i}$ to occur is at most $p^{\Size{x_{i}}}$.  Consider a correction algorithm that applies a correction $f$, such that $\partial^{-}(f + x) = 0$ and $f$ consists of clusters $\{f_{i}\}$ each of size at most $\Size{f_{i}} \le \frac{1}{2}\gamma \sqrt{n}$. Then the algorithm corrects the error.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  
\end{proof}
Suppose, through contradiction, that there is a cluster $y$ in $x+f$ which is not a stabilizer and has a size greater than $\gamma n$. Then $y$ consists of clusters $x_{1}, \ldots, x_{m_{1}}$ and $f_{1}, \ldots, f_{m_{2}}$, such that they are connected. Consider the spanning tree of the systems and group the $x_{i}$'s which are time-connected. Moreover, exclude the $f_{i}$'s which connect $x_{i}$ clusters that are connected in time.


Now, denote by $m$ the remained $x$'s. We have that the probability of the tree to occur is the minimum between $m$ and  
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    p^{\sum_{i}^{m} \Size{x_{i}}} \le p^{ \Size{y} - \sum_{i}^{m^{\prime}} \Size{f_{i}} } \le  p^{ \gamma n  - m^{\prime} \frac{1}{2}\gamma\sqrt{n} } 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}

Thus, if $m \ge \sqrt{n}$ then, .., and if lower than $\sqrt{n}$ then.., .

Since, any cluster in $f$ is lower than $\gamma^{\prime}n$ it follows that there is a cluster $x^{\prime}$ in $x$, that has a non-trivial intersection with $f$. Denote, by $f^{\prime}$ the minimal subset of $f$ such that $\partial^{-} (f^{\prime} + x^{\prime}) = 0$. Now, if $f^{\prime} + x^{\prime}$ is stabilizer, then just erase it from $y$, denote by $y^{\prime}$ the result of the erasing (if the erasing divide $y$ to multiple clusters then sets $y^{\prime}$ to the one with the maximal size). Repeat on the above until $f^{\prime}+x^{\prime}$ is not a stabilizer. Therefore, it has to be that $\Size{x^{\prime}+f^{\prime}} > \gamma n$. 
Now, take the components $f_{1},..,f_{d+1}$ such the maximal of $L_{i}$ is in $\Size{x^{\prime}+f^{\prime}}$ is got in $f_{i}$. Since $x^{\prime}+f^{\prime}$ is connected (otherwise $\partial^{-}(x^{\prime}+f^{\prime}) \neq 0$), and $f_{1},..,f_{d+1}$ are disconnected we have that: 


\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
 \sum\Size{f_{i}} &> \gamma n - \Size{x^{\prime}} > \gamma^{\prime} n\\
 \Rightarrow & \max \Size{f_{i}} > \frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{d+1} n
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}


\inb{\textbf{Even better.}} Assume that the clusters always have a size lower than $\gamma n$ and that there are $m$ bits. Run sweep-cycles for $m\gamma n$ times. Since there can be at most $m-1$ merges (since the number of clusters is bounded by the number of bits), there has to be a continuous sequence of $\gamma n$ cycles for which no merge occurs. Since it's given that the size of all the clusters is lower than $\gamma n$, we have that all the clusters are erased after those cycles. To prove that no cluster reaches a size greater than $\gamma n$, we use explanation trees (where trees in which all the leaves are at least $l$ from the root are contained in the trees without that restriction).
