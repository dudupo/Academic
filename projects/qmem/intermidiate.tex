

\subsection{Intermediate} 

The following definition of propagation describes the evolution of a fault. It assumes an order of steps and defines an operator that is obtained by commuting a given fault with the gates (and faults) prior to a given step.

\begin{definition}[Fault propagation to a step $\tau$]
  \label{def:localprop}
  Let $C=\{u_i\}_i$ be a quantum circuit, let $J=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)$ be an order agreeing with $\pi_C$, and let $u_i\in C$. Suppose that $u_i=u_{j_l}$ in this order. If $l>\tau$, we define the propagation of $u_i$ to step $\tau$ to be the identity operator.
Otherwise, define operators $\mathcal{D}_0,\mathcal{D}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{\tau-l}$ recursively by setting $\mathcal{D}_0=u_{j_l}$ and requiring that for every $1\le s\le \tau-l$,
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \mathcal{D}_{s-1}u_{j_{l+s}} = u_{j_{l+s}}\mathcal{D}_s.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  We then define the \emph{propagation of $u_i$ to step $\tau$} to be the operator
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \mathcal{D} \colonequals \mathcal{D}_{\tau-l}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
Now let $C[\mathcal{E}]$ be a noisy circuit, where all of its quantum registers are encoded in the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code with side length $n$. Let $J$ be an order agreeing with $\pi_{C[\mathcal{E}]}$, and let $\tau$ be a step in that order. For each faulty gate $e \in \mathcal{E}$ that occurs no later than step $\tau$, denote by $\mathcal{D}^{\tau}_{e}$ its propagation to step $\tau$. We define the propagated error at step $\tau$ by multiplying these propagated faults in reverse topological order.
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \mathcal{D}^{\tau} \colonequals \prod_{e\preceq \tau} \mathcal{D}^{\tau}_{e}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
Moreover, we say that a step $\tau$ admits an infinite error if there is a register such that, in the view of the $\infty$-extension of $C[\mathcal{E}]$, the Pauli decomposition of the propagated error at step $\tau$, $\mathcal{D}^{\tau}$, has a term with support on a connected, reduced, $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain $x$ such that $\Size{x} \ge n$.
\end{definition}


\inb{\textbf{Rewrite.}} The propagation up to step $\tau$ allows us to define the deviation from an ideal run by propagating all the faults and marking all the locations in the result. 

\inb{\textbf{Mention after the claim below, that we will take $\alpha$ such that the claim will also catch the dual code, namely set $d^{\prime} \leftarrow d - d^{\prime}$ if that decrease $\alpha$.}}

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:intersyn}
  Let $\alpha = (\Delta d^{\prime}(d-d^{\prime}+1))^{-1}$. Denote by $A$ the event that there is a step that admits an infinite error. In addition, for any step $\tau \in J$, denote by $B_{\tau}$ the event that step $\tau$ is the first step for which there is a register such that, in the view of the $\infty$-extension, $\mathcal{D}^{\tau}$ has support on a connected, reduced, $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain $x$ such that $\Size{x} = \alpha n$. Denote by $w$ and $\mu$ the width and the depth of $C[\mathcal{E}]$. Then the probability that $A$ occurs is bounded by:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \prb{A} \le w\mu\max_{\tau\in[w\mu]}\prb{B_{\tau}}.
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{claim}


\begin{proof}
  For the sake of the proof, we say that a chain $x$ is in operator $O$ if, when considering the Pauli decomposition of the image of $O$ in the $\infty$-extension, there is a correspondence between the faces of $x$ and the (partial) support of one of the terms in $O$'s image.

  Denote by $x$ the maximal, in size, reduced connected $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain in $\mathcal{D}^{\tau+1}$. Assume that $\tau$ is the first step to admits an infinite error, thus any chain in $\mathcal{D}^{\tau}$ has a size lower than $n$. Denote by $u$ the gates which is applied at step $\tau$, so there have to be Paulis $P_{1},P_{2}$ such that: 
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      P_{1} \in \text{Supp } \mathcal{D}^{\tau}, \, \,  P_{2} \in \text{Supp } \mathcal{D}^{\tau + 1}, \, \, x \text{ is both in } P_{1},P_{2}  \, \, \text{ and } \expval*{P_{1}|u|P_{2}} \neq 0
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
  Since $u$ has a fan at most $\Delta$, and since any qubit ($d^{\prime}$-dimensional face) is connected to at most $d^{\prime}(d-d^{\prime}+1)$ qubits (by stepping through a check\footnote{a $d^{\prime}-1$ dimensional face}), we get that the application of $u$ on $P_{1}$ touches at most $\alpha^{-1}$ chains, and the result of the modification yields $x$. Therefore, $x$ resides inside a union of at most $\alpha^{-1}$, and since $x$ is connected, we have that its size is bounded by summing the size of each chain in the union. Therefore, if any of the chains is at size at most $\alpha n$, it implies that the size of $x$ is lower than $n$. Hence, a necessary condition to step $\tau+1$ to be the first step to admit an infinite error is that step $\tau-1$ admits errors at size greater than $\alpha n$, and hence the existence of a step $\tau^{\prime}\le \tau$ such that $B_{\tau^{\prime}}$ occurs. The inequality follows by using the union bound over all the steps in $C[\mathcal{E}]$.
\end{proof}

We close the section by presenting Pauli-paths, which can be thought of as the collapsing errors due to stabilizer measurements.

\begin{definition} 
  \label{def:paulipath}
For a fixed quantum circuit $C$, where all its quantum registers are encoded with the Toric code, and a set of faults $\mathcal{E}$, such that the depth of $C[\mathcal{E}]$ is $\mu$, we will say that the Paulis $P_{1}, \ldots, P_{\mu+1} \in \mathcal{P}$ are fault paths of $C[\mathcal{E}]$ if $P_{1}$ is the identity and for every $t\le \mu+1$ the Pauli $P_{t+1}$ supports $C[\mathcal{E}]|_{t} P_{t}$. Namely:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \expval*{P_{t}|C[\mathcal{E}]|_{t}|P_{t+1}} \neq 0
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
We extend the notation to Pauli-path up to step $\tau$ by considering the quantum circuit obtained from $C[\mathcal{E}]$ by taking all the gates at steps lower than or equal to $\tau$. A Pauli-path of that circuit is a Pauli-path up to step $\tau$ of the original circuit.


Finally, we define a reduced Pauli-path $\tilde{P_{1}}, \ldots, \tilde{P_{\mu+1}}$ to be the Paulis obtained by taking a Pauli-path, considering its view in the $\infty$-extension, and for any finite connected chain that corresponds to a subset of the $X$ component of $P_{t}$'s support, substitute it with its reduced form (\Cref{def:reduced}). Then do the same for the $Z$ component in the dual picture.
\end{definition}
