







\section{Proof of the Theorem}

We are ready to prove the main theorem (\Cref{theorem:main}). We start by giving a formal definition of a simulation.

  \begin{definition}[Simulation and Logical Errors]
For a quantum code $\mathcal{C}$ of length $n$ and a decoder for $\mathcal{C}$, denoted by $D$, we say that a Pauli operator $P \in \mathcal{P}^{\otimes n}$ is a \textit{logical error} with respect to $D$ if $DP$ does not equal the logical identity.

For quantum circuits $C$ and $\tilde{C}$, a noise channel $N_{\mathcal{E}}$, and $p \in (0,1)$, we say that $\tilde{C}$ \textit{simulates} $C$ with probability $1 - p$ if
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item There is a mapping between the qubits of $C$ and the registers of $\tilde{C}$, along with a decoding procedure such that, at the end of running $\tilde{C}$ (on the zero state), decoding the registers results in the same quantum states as those produced by running $C$.
      \item Let $\mathcal{E} \sim N_{\mathcal{E}}$ be a set of faults. The probability induced by $N_{\mathcal{E}}$ that at the end of the running of $\tilde{C}[\mathcal{E}]$, one of its quantum registers introduces a logical error (with respect to the decoder) is less than $p$.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{definition}

  
  Our construction will result (after initialization) in a Toric encoded circuit, where all the quantum registers are encoded using the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code induced by a Toric with a side of length $n$. Then, for a decoder $D$, we are going to take the following simple procedure: just apply the sweep-cycle $n^{d+1} { d \choose d^{\prime} }  $ times. If we want him to correct a measurement, namely a classical state which is the outcome of the measurement, then we perform only the first stage of the sweep-cycle (without moving into the dual code). 

The following claim states that for ideal running (not subjected to noise), assuming that during the decoding all the connected errors (chain) are finite, the decoding succeeds in correcting them.

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:decodestopnoise}
  Let $\ket{\psi}$ be a logical codeword of the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code with side length $n$. Let $P \in \mathcal{P}^{(\cdot)}$, and consider a constant $\gamma \in (0,1)$. Assuming that during the application of $\gamma n^{d+1}{ d \choose d^{\prime} } $ ideal sweep-cycles over the $P\ket{\psi}$, the propagated error does not admit a reduced error (chain) of size greater than $\gamma n$, then at the end the error is erased, namely the result of the computation is $\ket{\psi}$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Since no new faults are introduced during the computation, we have that in each step the only option for a connected error to grow is by merging. However, there can be at most $n^{d}{ d \choose d^{\prime} } -1$ merges (since the number of connected components is bounded by the number of bits), and hence there has to be a continuous sequence of $\gamma n$ cycles for which no merge occurs. Since it's given that the size of all the connected errors is lower than $\gamma n$, and a sweep-cycle decreases the size of a finite error by at least 1, we have that all the connected errors are erased after those cycles.
\end{proof}
To prove that no connected error reaches a size greater than $\gamma n$, we use explanation trees. In particular, an explanation tree that characterizes the running well is one that, prior to decoding, allows the introduction of new faults, and therefore leaves, and during the ideal $\gamma n^{d+1}{ d \choose d^{\prime} } $ iterations, no new leaf is introduced. The way to analyze the probability of a proper explanation occurring is to bound it by the probability\footnote{Actually, by an upper bound of the probability} of the same explanation occurring in the setting where the following $\gamma n^{d+1}{ d \choose d^{\prime} } $ iterations are also subjected to local stochastic noise. 

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:replace}
  Consider a rapid application of the sweep-cycle for $l$ times. Denote by $A$ the event in which, after an additional $n^{d+1}$ \textbf{ideal} (non-noisy) iterations of the sweep-cycle, the error is not erased, and denote by $B$ the event that, during the application of an additional $n^{d+1}$ \textbf{noisy} iterations, the propagated error addmits an infinite error. Let $f:(n,d,d^{\prime},l,p) \rightarrow \mathbb{R} $ be an upper bound on $\prb{B}$ that assumes only that the noise is distributed according to a local stochastic noise channel with noise rate $p$. Then, we have:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \prb{A} \le f(n,d,d^{\prime},l,p)
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof} 
\Cref{claim:decodestopnoise} states that if during the $n^{d+1}$ ideal sweep-cycles the propagated error does not admit an infinite error, then the error is erased. Consider the set of atomic events corresponding to the faults in the first $l$ noisy iterations, such that during the additional $n^{d+1}$ ideal iterations there is a step that admits an infinite error. All those atomic events are also contained in $B$, since local stochastic noise is our only assumption. It's possible that noise in the $B$ scenario is distributed such that the probability of a fault occurring during the additional $n^{d+1}$ iterations is zero. In that specific case, $\prb{A} = \prb{B}$ and therefore $\prb{A} \le f(n,d,d^{\prime},l,p)$.
\end{proof}

The next lemma is the core of the theorem; it analyzes the probability of a logical error occurring after the initialization stage, namely when assuming the given input is encoded.

  \begin{lemma}
For any $d, d^{\prime}$, there are constants $a, b \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$ and $p_{0} \in (0,1)$ that depend only on $d, d^{\prime}$ and satisfy the following. Consider a quantum circuit $C$ with depth $\mu$ and width $w$, and let $\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}}$ be a local stochastic noise channel with a noise rate $p < p_{0}$. There is a Toric encoded circuit $\tilde{C}$ such that each of its quantum registers is encoded by an $n$-side length, $(d, d^{\prime})$-Toric code, and it simulates $C$ with probability:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    q = 1 -   \Theta \left(((w+2\mu)\mu n^{2})^{3} \right) \left( a p^{b} \right)^{ \frac{1}{d}} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

To prove the lemma, we will need the following two claims. The first bounds, for a given regular graph, the number of its subgraphs having at most $k$ edges. This claim will be used to infer the number of possible explanation trees for a given slice. The second states that each tree has a subtree with fewer than half of the edges and a leaves-to-edge ratio that is at least half of the ratio of the original tree. This claim will be used to infer that any explanation has a subtree where, under the projection to the finite Toric, its faults are mapped uniquely, meaning no fault is counted twice. 

\begin{claim*}[\Cref{claim:counting_trees}]
Let $G = \left( V, E \right)$ be a $\Delta + 1$-regular graph, and let $k$ be an integer less than $|E|$. Fix a vertex $r \in V$. There is a constant number $\sigma$, depending only on $\Delta$, such that the number of subgraphs rooted at $r$ with fewer than $k$ edges is at most $\sigma^{k}$.
  \end{claim*}

  \begin{claim*}[\Cref{claim:big_subtree}]
Let $\gamma, \beta \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ and $n$ be a positive integer. For any tree $T$ with $\tau \ge \beta n$ edges and $m$ leaves such that $\tau \le \gamma m$, there is a subtree $T^{\prime}$ of $T$ with a number of edges $\tau^{\prime}$ less than $\frac{\beta}{2}n$ and a number of leaves greater than $\frac{\beta}{4\gamma} n$.
  \end{claim*}
  
The proofs of the claims are given in the appendix. We continue with the proof of the lemma.
  \begin{proof}
    We construct $\tilde{C}$ as follows: for each input wire of $C$, we initialize the logical zero $\ket{\mathbf{0}}$ of the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code. For each of the gates $\mathbf{H}$ and $\mathbf{S}$, we initialize the encoded $\ket{\mathbf{H}}$ and $\ket{\mathbf{S}}$ states, and for each $\mathbf{T}$, we initialize copies of both $\ket{\mathbf{T}}$ and $\ket{\mathbf{S}}$ states. The even layers of $\tilde{C}$ are sweep cycles, while on the odd layers, we set the realization of the logical gates. For $\mathbf{CX}$, we set the transversal realization, and for $\mathbf{H}$, $\mathbf{S}$, and $\mathbf{T}$, we use gate teleportation. Thus the width of $\tilde{C}$ expands to at most $ (w + 2\mu) n^{dd^{\prime}}$.  For gate teleportation, we use the decoder in \Cref{claim:decodestopnoise}, namely an application of $n^{d+1}$ sweep cycles, followed by a classical logarithmic depth procedure that infers a logical measurement and might apply a second gate conditioned on it. Therefore, the gate teleportation admits a $\Theta(n^{d+1} \log n)$ depth-overhead. Thus, the depth of $\tilde{C}$ expands to $\Theta(\mu n^{d+1} \log n)$\footnote{The constant inside the $\Theta$ also accounts for the fact that the realization of a logical gate might contain multiple gate teleportations.}. Denote by $S$ the number of locations, so $S \le \Theta(w \mu^{2} n^{(d+1)dd^{\prime}} \log n)$.
  
    

    If there is no application in which the decoder fails, then clearly the corrections followed in the gate teleportation procedures are the right ones, namely the logical gates that were applied simulate the gates in the original circuit. To ensure that $D$ succeeds in decoding the output states, it's enough to imagine that there are more than $n$ additional ideal sweep-cycles at the end and require them to realize a successful decoding. 

    \Cref{claim:decodestopnoise} states that the $D$ successfully decodes assuming that no infinite error occurs. And \Cref{claim:replace} states that it's sufficient to bound the probability that no infinite error occurs by any upper bound that assumes only a local stochastic behavior on the probability that no infinite error occurs in the running where the decoding procedures are subjected to noise\footnote{Observe that in this setting, the classical procedure that infers a logical measurement makes no sense}. Thus, it remains to prove that it happens with probability $q$. No infinite error is admitted in the setting in which the decoding procedures are also subjected to noise. From now on, we consider that setting.  

Let $\alpha = (\Delta d^{\prime}(d-d^{\prime}+1))^{-1}$. \Cref{claim:intersyn} states that the probability of there being a time $t$ (or a step) that admits an infinite error is bounded by the number of locations multiplied by the probability that step $\tau$ is the first to admit a finite connected error (chain) in the propagated error at size greater than $\alpha n$, maximized over $\tau$. Notice that if $\tau$ is the first step to admit an error at size $\alpha n$, then all the slices in analysis of Toom's contour at step $\tau$ have an explanation. 


Denote by $B_{\tau}$ the event where $\tau$ is the first step to admit a finite connected error of size greater than $\alpha n$, and by $A_{\tau}$ the event where all explanations of slices up to and including step $\tau$ have fewer than $\frac{n}{d}$ edges. Thus:
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \prbm{  B_{\tau}    }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} } & =  \prbm{  B_{\tau}  \cap A_{\tau}  }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} } + \prbm{  B_{\tau} \cap \bar{A}_{\tau}    }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} }  \\
& \le \prbm{  B_{\tau}  | A_{\tau}  }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} } + \prbm{  \bar{A}_{\tau} | B_{\tau}   }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} }  
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}

    
    We begin by bounding the contribution of the right term. Conditioning on $B_{\tau}$ guarantees that any slice has an explanation. According to \Cref{coro:edgefaultratio}, there exists a constant $c_{1}$, depending only on $d$, such that the ratio of leaves to edges in the explanation is at least $c_{1}$. By \Cref{claim:big_subtree}, it follows that any such explanation has more than $\frac{n}{d}$ edges containing a subtree with fewer than $\frac{n}{2d}$ edges, with a leaves-to-edges ratio greater than $\frac{1}{2} c_{1}$. Using the union bound, we bound the probability that there is a vertex in the base graph that is a root of such a subtree. Since the construction of the $\infty$-extension sets periodic conditions, it's sufficient to ask if there is such a vertex in the restriction to the finite $n$-side length Toric.
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \prbm{  \bar{A}_{\tau} | B_{\tau}   }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} }  \le  \bigcup_{ \substack{ v \in V_{o}, \tau^{\prime} \le \tau \\ L_{g}(v) \in [0,n]} } \prbm{ & v \text{ is a root of subtree in } \mathcal{H}_{\tau^{\prime}}  \\ 
      &  \text{ with less than  } \frac{n}{2d} \text{ edges and more than } \frac{1}{2}c_{1} \frac{n}{2d} n \text{ leaves}  }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} } 
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
    Now, since any two connected vertices are at a space distance of at most 
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \max\{\Size{\text{arrow}}, \Size{\text{fork}}\} \le d^{\prime},d-d^{\prime} \le d
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
    It follows that all the vertices in the subtree are at a space distance of at most $\frac{1}{2} n$ from each other. Thus, no two of them have the same space coordinate modulo $n$. In particular, projecting the infinite plane back to the finite Toric sends each of the leaves of the subtree to a unique target, and therefore the number of faults that induce the explanation is at least:
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \frac{1}{2^{\max\{d^{\prime}, d - d^{\prime}\}}} \frac{1}{4} c_{1} \frac{n}{d} \ge \frac{1}{2^{d}} \frac{1}{2} c_{1} \alpha \frac{n}{d} 
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
Plugging into the union bounds and combining \Cref{claim:counting_trees} yields:
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \prbm{  \bar{A}_{\tau} | B_{\tau}   }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} }  \le \tau S \sigma^{  \frac{n}{2d} } p^{ \frac{c_{1}n}{2^{d+2} d } }\le S^{2} \left( \sigma p^{\frac{1}{2^{d + 1}} c_{1}} \right)^{\frac{n}{2d}} 
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}


It remains to bound the probability of $B_{\tau}| A_{\tau}$. Since $\tau$ is the first step that admits an error with size at least $\alpha n$, it follows that any slice has an explanation. Conditioned on the event $A_{\tau}$, any explanation implies that any explanation has fewer than $\frac{n}{d}$ edges and therefore, by the same argument as above, the projection of each explanation tree into the finite Toric sends its leaves to unique targets.

    Using \Cref{claim:edgesin} again, any explanation of a slice of size $\alpha n$ has at least $c_{1} \alpha n$ edges. Therefore, it suffices to bound the probability that the 'analysis Toom's contour', $\mathcal{H}_{\tau}$ admits an explanation with more than $c_{1}\alpha n$ edges. Hence, we obtain:
    \begin{equation*}
      \begin{split}
        \prbm{  B_{\tau}  | A_{\tau}  }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} } & \le \bigcup_{ \substack{ v \in V_{o}, x \ge c_{1}\alpha n \\ L_{g}(v) \in [0,n]} } \prbm{ v \text{ is a root of explanation with number of edges } x  | A_{\tau}  }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} } \\
        & \le \sum_{x = c_{1}\alpha n }^{ \infty } S \left( \sigma p^{\frac{1}{2^{d}} c_{1}} \right)^{x}  = S \frac{\left( \sigma p^{\frac{1}{2^{d }} c_{1}} \right)^{\alpha n}}{ 1 -  \left( \sigma p^{\frac{1}{2^{d }} c_{1}} \right)} 
      \end{split}
    \end{equation*}
Combing the bounds we have:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
\prbm{  B_{\tau}    }{ \sim \mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{E}} } \le S^{2} \left( \sigma p^{\frac{1}{2^{d + 1}} c_{1}} \right)^{\frac{n}{2d}} + S \frac{\left( \sigma p^{\frac{1}{2^{d }} c_{1}} \right)^{\alpha n}}{ 1 -  \left( \sigma p^{\frac{1}{2^{d }} c_{1}} \right)} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}

Now, combined with \Cref{claim:intersyn}, there are constants $c_{2}, c_{3}$ depending only on $d, d^{\prime}$ such that with a probability greater than:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    1 -   \Theta \left( S^{3} \right) \left( c_{3} p^{c_{2}} \right)^{ \frac{n}{2d} } 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
No step admits an infinite error, so the decoder successfully restores the logical state; in other words, no logical error occurs.
\end{proof}
The lemma gives the theorem almost immediately. To complete the proof, we need to plug in the initialization phase, which initializes encoded zeros and resource states subjected to local stochastic noise. We emphasize that the resulting states are subjected to local stochastic noise (in addition to the computation). We neither prove nor describe that phase here; instead, we refer to \cite{grospellier:tel-03364419}, \cite{gottesman2014faulttolerant}, \cite{Fawzi_2018}, and \cite{nguyen2025quantumfaulttoleranceconstantspace}.
  \begin{theorem}
    \label{theorem:main}
  There exists a constant $p_{0} \in (0,1)$ such that for any local stochastic noise channel $\mathcal{E}$ with rate $p < p_{0}$, and a quantum circuit $C$ with depth $\mu$ and width $w$, there exists a Toric encoded circuit $\tilde{C}$, with depth $\Theta(\mu n)$ and width $\Theta((w + \mu) n)$  that simulates $C$ with probability: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    1 -   \Theta \left( \text{poly}(w,\mu,n) \right)  p^{\Theta(n^{\frac{1}{4}})} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
and in addition: 
\begin{enumerate}
  \item After a short initialization stage (at depth $\Theta(\log n)$), each of the qubits is encoded in the $n$-length 4D Toric code.
  \item An error correction cycle takes $O(1)$ depth, and the gates in each correction cycle don't depend on time. In particular, if $C$ is the identity circuit, then after the initialization stage, the layers in $\tilde{C}$ are the same.
\end{enumerate}
  \end{theorem}
