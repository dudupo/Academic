



\subsection{Sweep Rule}

The sweep-rule is the core of our construction. A decoding cycle begins with each of the vertices reading the syndrome on its positive $X$-checks (checks that are associated with the positive $d^{\prime}-1$ dimensional faces of the vertex) and, based on what it sees, applying a Pauli correction. The sweep-rule defines the correction to be over the positive qubits such that it will turn off the positive syndrome. It's not hard to see that for the $(2,2)$-Toric complex, the Toom's and the sweep rules are the same. Compared to other correction cycles on topological codes, the main advantage of the sweep is that it is independent of time. The invention of the sweep-rule is attributed to Aleksander Kubica and John Preskill \cite{Kubica_2019}. Here we extend their work to cubical complexes.

\begin{definition}[Sweep Rule]
  \label{def:sweeprule}
  Let $\sigma$ be a $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain. The \textit{sweep rule decoding procedure} at a vertex $v \in \mathcal{G}_{n}^{d}$ is defined as follows: Measure the checks rooted at $v$ and look for a rooted chain $\tilde{z}$ at $v$ such that: 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The vertices in $\tilde{z}$ are contained in the vertices of $\sigma$, namely: $ \mathbf{ln}_{0+}(v, \tilde{z}) \subset \mathbf{ln}_{0+}(v, \sigma)$ % . Any $d^{\prime}$ dimensional face $\rotfvs{}$ in $\tilde{z}$, satisfies $ (\partial^{-})^{d^{\prime}}\rotfvs{} \subset  \partial^{-}(z)|_{v+}$. 
    \item The boundary of $\tilde{z}$ agrees with $\sigma$ on the positive checks of $v$, namely: $(\partial^{-}\tilde{z})|_{v+} = \sigma|_{v+}$.
  \end{enumerate}
  If such an assignment exists, flip $\tilde{z}$. \inb{Write again. Consider the update}. 
\end{definition}
% \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}-1}}$

The first property is analogous to the second property of 'causal structure' defined in \cite{Kubica_2019}\footnote{In their language: $\diamondsuit(\tilde{z}) = \diamondsuit(\sigma|_{v})$.}. There, they conjectured that vertices that see a syndrome only on their positive checks can suggest a correction that is 'supported' on the vertices of their unsatisfied checks. For lower dimensions, they mentioned that the property can be easily checked. Here, we provide a proof for any dimensions where the face dimension is greater than one. We use the language of cubical complexes; however, since the 'positive' local view at a vertex is identical for both cubical and simplicial complexes, generalizing the proof for simplicial complexes is a matter of adjusting notations. By that, we affirmatively answer their conjecture. Moreover, the proof will provide us with an algorithm to find $\tilde{z}$, which is significantly better than running all options.

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:findcor}
Consider a nontrivial $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain $\sigma$. If there is a $d$-dimensional chain $z$ that is rooted at $v$ and whose boundary agrees with $\sigma|_{v+}$, then there is a $d$-dimensional chain $\tilde{z}$ rooted at $v$ that satisfies the sweep-rule conditions.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof} 
  We prove it by induction on $d^{\prime}$. 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Base. Consider $d^{\prime} = 2$ and let $d$ be any integer equal to or greater than $3$. We argue that the support size of $\sigma$ has to be even. To see that, denote the faces in $z$ by $\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}_{i}}$, where $S^{\prime}_{i} = \{g_{i,1},g_{i,2}\}$. Using the fact that $\sigma$ is the boundary of $z$, we have:
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \sigma = \partial^{-}(z) = \sum_{ \rotfvs{} \in z}\sum_{ g \in S^{\prime} } \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime } / g } = \sum_{ i } \rotfv{v}{ \{g_{i,1}\} }+ \rotfv{v}{ \{g_{i,2}\} } 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
So the number of terms in the sum is even, and since a term is zeroed out only if it appears an even number of times, we have that after excluding the vanishing terms, $\sigma$ has an even number of faces. Since the number of faces is even, there is a perfect matching between them. It's easy to see that the $2$-chain $\tilde{z}$, whose each face corresponds to the union of the two edges in a match, satisfies the sweep-rule conditions.
    \item Assume the correctness of the claim for every face dimension lower than $d^{\prime}$.   Now for a face dimension $d^{\prime}$ and an arbitrary generator $g \in S$ such $gv \in \sigma$, divide $\sigma$ into a sum of two chains, the first contain the faces of $\sigma$ that supported on the vertex $gv$ where the second contains the compliment. Denote them by $\sigma|_{gv}$ and $\sigma|_{/ gv}$, so $\sigma = \sigma|_{gv} + \sigma|_{/ gv}$. 

      Now let $ \sigma^{*}$ the chain obtained from $\sigma|_{gv}$ by removing from any face of it the vertex $gv$, thus $\sigma^{*}$ is a $d^{\prime}-2$-dimensional chain. Moreover, let $z^{*}$ be the $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain complex obtained from $z|_{gv}$ by excluding $gv$ from its faces. Observes that 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \partial^{-} z^{*} =  \sum_{\rotfvs \in z|_{gv}}\sum_{g^{\prime} \in S^{\prime}/g}\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}/\{g,g^{\prime}\}} = \sum_{\rotfvs \in (\partial^{-} z)|_{gv}}\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}/\{g\}} = \sigma^{*}
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
      Thus, by the induction assumption, there is a $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain $\tilde{z}^{*}$ such that: 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $ \mathbf{ln}_{0+}(v, \tilde{z}^{*}) \subset \mathbf{ln}_{0+}(v, \sigma^{*})$  
    \item $(\partial^{-}\tilde{z}^{*})|_{v+} = \sigma^{*}|_{v+}$
  \end{enumerate}
The first condition implies that $gv \notin \tilde{z}^{*}$. Hence, we can define the $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain $\tilde{z}$ by adding the vertex $gv$ to each face of $\tilde{z}^{*}$. From the second it follows that: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \partial^{-} \tilde{z} &= \partial^{-} \bigl(  \sum_{\rotfvs \in \tilde{z}^{*} } \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}\cup \{g\}}  \bigr) =  \sum_{\rotfvs \in \tilde{z}^{*} } \bigl( \sum_{g^{\prime} \in S^{\prime}/g } \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime} / \{g^{\prime}\} \cup \{g\}} \bigr) +   \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}} \\
    &=  \sum_{\rotfvs \in \partial^{-}\tilde{z}^{*} } \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}\cup \{g\}} + \tilde{z}^{*} = \sigma|_{gv} + \tilde{z}^{*} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Therefore, 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \sigma + \partial^{-} \tilde{z} &= \sigma|_{gv} + \sigma|_{/ gv} + \sigma|_{gv} + \tilde{z}^{*} \\ 
    &= \sigma|_{/ gv} + \tilde{z}^{*}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Thus we reduce the question to handle the $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain $\sigma + \partial^{-} \tilde{z}$. However, since $gv \notin  \sigma + \partial^{-} \tilde{z}$ it follows that: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    |\mathbf{ln}_{0+}(v, \sigma + \partial^{-} \tilde{z})| \le  |\mathbf{ln}_{0+}(v, \sigma )| - 1
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Thus, we can repeat recursively until the resulting $d^{\prime}-1$ chain is 'supported' on fewer than $d^{\prime}$ vertices. In that case, the chain must be trivial.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

A full decoding cycle is composed of first applying the sweep-rule regarding the $X$-syndrome, then moving into the dual code (and reordering the qubits) by $\mathbf{H}_{\phi}$, and then applying the sweep-rule again with the change that the positive direction is flipped to be the negative direction. At the end, we return to the original code by applying $\mathbf{H}_{\phi}$.

\begin{definition}[Sweep cycle]
We define the \textit{sweep cycle} to be the following procedure:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For each vertex, perform the sweep rule.
    \item Apply the Hadamard transform $\mathbf{H}^{\otimes n}$ followed by the $\phi$-transpose, and flip the positive direction.
    \item Again, apply the sweep rule at each vertex.
    \item Reverse stage 2 by reordering the qubits according to the $\phi$-transpose, and then apply the Hadamard transform $\mathbf{H}^{\otimes n}$. Flip the positive direction again.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}


To have our constructions yield circuits that have $\infty$-extensions \Cref{def:extension}, the sweep-rule cycles have to be 'decoding gadgets' \Cref{def:decgate}.  
\begin{claim}
  The local gates that perform the sweep rule are decoding gadgets.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  A local sweep update acts on a constant-size neighborhood around a vertex, uses only local syndrome information, and may be implemented with fresh ancillas that are reset after the update. Any dependence on faults inside the gadget can therefore be pushed onto the data wires as an effective Pauli operator supported on the encoded register, while the reset ancillas carry no residual information. This is exactly the form required in the definition of a decoding gadget.
\end{proof}
