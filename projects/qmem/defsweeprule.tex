



\subsection{Sweep Rule}

The sweep-rule is the core of our construction. A decoding cycle begins with each vertex $v$ reading the syndrome on the $X$-checks rooted at $v$ (the positive $X$-checks of $v$) and, based on what it sees, applying a Pauli correction supported on the qubits rooted at $v$. The sweep-rule defines the correction to be over the positive qubits such that it will turn off the positive syndrome. It's not hard to see that for the $(2,2)$-Toric complex, the Toom's and the sweep rules are the same. Compared to other correction cycles on topological codes, the main advantage of the sweep is that it is independent of time. The invention of the sweep-rule is attributed to Aleksander Kubica and John Preskill \cite{Kubica_2019}. 

\begin{definition}[Sweep Rule]
  \label{def:sweeprule}
  Let $\sigma$ be a $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain. The \textit{sweep rule decoding procedure} at a vertex $v \in \mathcal{G}_{n}^{d}$ is defined as follows: Measure the checks rooted at $v$ and look for a rooted chain $\tilde{z}$ at $v$ such that: 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The vertices in $\tilde{z}$ are contained in the vertices of $\sigma|_{v+}$, namely: $ \mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \tilde{z}) \subseteq \mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \sigma|_{v})$ % . Any $d^{\prime}$ dimensional face $\rotfvs{}$ in $\tilde{z}$, satisfies $ (\partial^{-})^{d^{\prime}}\rotfvs{} \subset  \partial^{-}(z)|_{v+}$. 
    \item The boundary of $\tilde{z}$ agrees with $\sigma$ on the checks rooted at $v$, namely: $(\partial^{-}\tilde{z})|_{v+} = \sigma|_{v+}$.
  \end{enumerate}
  If such an assignment exists, flip $\tilde{z}$.
\end{definition}
% \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}-1}}$

The first property is analogous to the second property of 'causal structure' defined in \cite{Kubica_2019}\footnote{In their language: $\diamondsuit(\tilde{z}) = \diamondsuit(\sigma|_{v})$.}. There, they conjectured that vertices that see a syndrome only on their positive checks can suggest a correction that is 'supported' on the vertices of their unsatisfied checks. For lower dimensions, they mentioned that the property can be easily checked. Here, we provide a proof for any dimensions where the face dimension is greater than one. We use the language of cubical complexes; however, since the 'positive' local view at a vertex is identical for both cubical and simplicial complexes, generalizing the proof for simplicial complexes is a matter of adjusting notations. By that, we affirmatively answer their conjecture. Moreover, the proof will provide us with an algorithm to find $\tilde{z}$, which is significantly better than running all options.

\begin{claim}
  \label{claim:findcor}
  For $d^{\prime}\ge 2$, consider a nontrivial $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain $\sigma$. If there is a $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain $z$ that is rooted at $v$ and whose boundary agrees with $\sigma|_{v+}$, then there is a $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain $\tilde{z}$ rooted at $v$ that satisfies the sweep-rule conditions.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof} 
We prove it by induction on $d^{\prime}$. Without loss of generality, we can assume that $\sigma$ has support only over the positive faces of $v$, namely $\sigma|_{v+} = \sigma$.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Base. Consider $d^{\prime} = 2$ and let $d$ be any integer equal to or greater than $3$. We argue that the support size of $\sigma$ has to be even. To see that, denote the faces in $z$ by $\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}_{i}}$, where $S^{\prime}_{i} = \{g_{i,1},g_{i,2}\}$. Using the fact that $\sigma$ is the boundary of $z$ at $v$, we have:
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          \sigma = (\partial^{-}(z))|_{v} = \sum_{ \rotfvs{} \in z}\sum_{ g \in S^{\prime} } \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime } / g } = \sum_{ i } \rotfv{v}{ \{g_{i,1}\} }+ \rotfv{v}{ \{g_{i,2}\} } 
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
So the number of terms in the sum is even, and since a term is zeroed out only if it appears an even number of times, we have that after excluding the vanishing terms, $\sigma$ has an even number of faces. Since the number of faces is even, there is a perfect matching between them. It's easy to see that the $2$-chain $\tilde{z}$, each of whose faces corresponds to the union of the two edges in a match, satisfies the sweep-rule conditions.
    \item Assume the correctness of the claim for every face dimension lower than $d^{\prime}$.   Now for a face dimension $d^{\prime}$ and an arbitrary generator $g \in S$ such $gv \in \sigma$, divide $\sigma$ into a sum of two chains, the first contains the faces of $\sigma$ that are supported on the vertex $gv$ while the second contains the complement. Denote them by $\sigma|_{gv}$ and $\sigma|_{/ gv}$, so $\sigma = \sigma|_{gv} + \sigma|_{/ gv}$. 

      Now let $ \sigma^{*}$ be the chain obtained from $\sigma|_{gv}$ by removing from any face of it the vertex $gv$, thus $\sigma^{*}$ is a $d^{\prime}-2$-dimensional chain. Moreover, let $z^{*}$ be the $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain obtained from $z|_{gv}$ by excluding $gv$ from its faces. Observe that 
      \begin{equation*}
        \begin{split}
          (\partial^{-} z^{*})|_{v} =  \sum_{\rotfvs \in z|_{gv}}\sum_{g^{\prime} \in S^{\prime}/g}\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}/\{g,g^{\prime}\}} = \sum_{\rotfvs \in (\partial^{-} z)|_{gv}}\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}/\{g\}} = \sigma^{*}
        \end{split}
      \end{equation*}
      Thus, by the induction assumption, there is a $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain $\tilde{z}^{*}$ such that: 
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $ \mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \tilde{z}^{*}) \subseteq \mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \sigma^{*}|_{v})$  
    \item $(\partial^{-}\tilde{z}^{*})|_{v+} = \sigma^{*}|_{v+}$
  \end{enumerate}
The first condition implies that $gv \notin \tilde{z}^{*}$. Hence, we can define the $d^{\prime}$-dimensional chain $\tilde{z}$ by adding the vertex $gv$ to each face of $\tilde{z}^{*}$. From the second it follows that: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    (\partial^{-} \tilde{z})|_{v} &= \bigl(\partial^{-} \bigl(  \sum_{\rotfvs \in \tilde{z}^{*} } \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}\cup \{g\}}  \bigr)\bigr)|_{v} =  \sum_{\rotfvs \in \tilde{z}^{*} } \bigl( \sum_{g^{\prime} \in S^{\prime}/g } \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime} / \{g^{\prime}\} \cup \{g\}}  +   \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}} \bigr)\\
    &=  \sum_{\rotfvs \in (\partial^{-}\tilde{z}^{*})|_{v} } \rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}\cup \{g\}} + \tilde{z}^{*} = \sigma|_{gv} + \tilde{z}^{*} 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Therefore, 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    \sigma + (\partial^{-} \tilde{z})|_{v} &= \sigma|_{gv} + \sigma|_{/ gv} + \sigma|_{gv} + \tilde{z}^{*} \\ 
    &= \sigma|_{/ gv} + \tilde{z}^{*}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Thus we reduce the question to handle the $d^{\prime}-1$-dimensional chain $\sigma + (\partial^{-} \tilde{z})|_{v}$. However, since $gv \notin  \sigma + (\partial^{-} \tilde{z})|_{v}$ it follows that: 
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    |\mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \sigma + (\partial^{-} \tilde{z})|_{v} \, )| \le  |\mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \sigma )| - 1
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Thus, we can repeat recursively until the resulting $d^{\prime}-1$ chain is 'supported' on fewer than $d^{\prime}$ vertices. In that case, the chain must be trivial. That is because the resulting chain is the $\partial^{-}$-boundary of the rooted chain $z + \tilde{z}$ at vertex $v$ restricted to the positive faces of $v$. So, any nontrivial $d^{\prime}-1$ dimensional face in the resulting $d^{\prime}-1$ chain introduces $d^{\prime}$ vertices, one of them is $v$ itself, and the other $d^{\prime}-1$ are vertices in $\mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \sigma + (\partial^{-} \tilde{z})|_{v} \, )$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proof}
The algorithm given in the proof of \Cref{claim:findcor} for finding $\tilde{z}$ is as follows: given $\sigma$, pick a $g \in S$ such that $gv \in \sigma$. Then, define $\sigma^{*}$ by removing $gv$ from each face of $\sigma|_{gv}$. Let $\tilde{z}^{*}$ be the result of the recursive call on $\sigma^{*}$, and return '$\tilde{z}^{*} \cup \{gv\}$' plus the recursive call on $\sigma|_{/gv} + \tilde{z}^{*}$.

We stop the recursion whenever either $d^{\prime}=2$ (returning the perfect-matching 2-chain from the base case of \Cref{claim:findcor} ) or $|\mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \sigma|_{v})| < d^{\prime}-1$ (returning $0$). If the $\partial^{-}$-boundary of the returned chain doesn't match $\sigma|_{v+}$, then there is no chain that satisfies the sweep rule constraints. Otherwise, the returned chain is a valid sweep-rule suggestion.

The work in each recursion step consists of decomposing $\sigma$ into $\sigma|_{gv}$ and $\sigma|_{/gv}$ and summing ${ d \choose d^{\prime} }$-length vectors. We summarize by introducing the following claim which gives the running time of the algorithm in the RAM computation model.
\begin{claim}
  Let $ T(d, d', k) : \mathbb{N}^{\times 3} \rightarrow \mathbb{R} $ be the upper bound function for the running time of the algorithm given in \Cref{claim:findcor}, where $d$ is the dimension of the skeleton, $d^{\prime}$ is the face dimension, and $k$ is the number of vertices supporting the local view, namely $k = |\mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \sigma|_{v} )|$. Then:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      T(d, d', k) = O\bigl( 2^{k} \max_{d^{\star}\le d^{\prime}}{ d \choose d^{\star} } \bigr)
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
  In each recursive call, the vertex support of $\sigma$ matches a different subset of the vertices in $\mathbf{Str}_{0+}(v, \sigma|_{v})$; thus, the number of recursive calls is at most the number of subsets, namely $2^{k}$. The decomposition of $\sigma$ into $\sigma|_{gv}$ and $\sigma|_{/gv}$, and the summation $\sigma|_{/gv} + \tilde{z}^{*}$ are linear in the vector's length, namely linear in $\max_{d^{\star} \le d^{\prime} } {d \choose d^{\star}}$. So we bound the total work by the number of recursive steps multiplied by the maximal work at each recursive step. That becomes at most:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
    T(d,d^{\prime},k) \le O\bigl( 2^{k} \max_{d^{\star} \le d^{\prime}} { d \choose d^{\star} } \bigr) 
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
 %Finally, choose $c$, such we get correctness in the base case, namely  $k,d^{\prime} \le 4$ and any $d$. 
\end{proof}

Later (see \Cref{def:trans} and \Cref{def:htrans}), we will see that an isomorphism between the $(d,d^{\prime})$-Toric code and its dual, which is the $(d,d-d^{\prime})$-Toric code, can be realized by transversal Hadamards followed by the following permutation over the qubits.
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{split}
      \phi(\rotfv{v}{S^{\prime}}) \rightarrow \rotfv{ S^{\prime}v }{S/S^{\prime}} 
    \end{split}
  \end{equation*}
A full decoding cycle consists of four steps: first, applying the sweep rule regarding the $X$-syndrome; next, moving to the dual code; then applying the sweep rule again; and finally getting back to the code.
\begin{definition}[Sweep cycle]
We define the \textit{sweep cycle} to be the following procedure:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For each vertex, perform the sweep rule.
    \item Apply the Hadamard transform $\mathbf{H}^{\otimes\mathcal{F}_{d^\prime}^{n}}$ followed by the $\phi$-transpose.
    \item Again, apply the sweep rule at each vertex.
    \item Reverse stage 2 by reordering the qubits according to the $\phi^{-1}$-transpose, and then apply the Hadamard transform $\mathbf{H}^{\otimes\mathcal{F}_{d^\prime}^{n}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
